## The O/L English Language examination at a glance

Verified from the official 2020 marking scheme (Department of Examinations). Details of every test are in **English_Past_Papers.md**.

| Paper | Tests | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Paper I | Tests 1–8: reading, vocabulary, grammar, a notice (about 40–50 words) and a paragraph (about 50–60 words) | 40 |
| Paper II | Tests 9–16: vocabulary, grammar, cloze, reading, a letter or chart description (about 100 words) and an essay/article/speech/dialogue (about 200 words) | 60 |

Writing marks are given for **content, language, organisation/format and mechanics** (spelling, punctuation, handwriting). If content is 0, the whole writing answer gets 0.

### Level 1 — Subject and verb

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-A01","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"A","level":1,"difficulty":"Beginner"} -->

**Pattern:** Subject + Verb

The **subject** is who or what does the action. The **verb** is the action. With *he, she, it* or one person (*Kamal*), add **-s** or **-es** to the verb.

*தமிழ்:* எழுவாய் (யார்?) + வினை (என்ன செய்கிறார்?)

**Examples:**

- I study.
- He runs.
- She reads.
- They play.
- We eat.
- Birds fly.
- The baby sleeps.
- My father works.
- Dogs bark.
- The sun shines.

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-A02","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"A","level":2,"difficulty":"Beginner"} -->

**Pattern:** Subject + Verb + Object

The **object** answers *what?* or *whom?* after the verb: *He plays* **what?** → *cricket*.

*தமிழ்:* செயப்படுபொருள் — 'எதை?' / 'யாரை?'

**Examples:**

- I study English.
- He plays cricket.
- She reads books.
- They watch television.
- We eat rice.
- My mother cooks dinner.
- The farmer grows paddy.
- Nimal helps his father.
- The children drink milk.
- Our teacher teaches Science.

### Level 3 — Adding time and place

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-A03","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"A","level":3,"difficulty":"Beginner"} -->

**Pattern:** Subject + Verb + Object + Place + Time

Put the **place** (*at home, in the library*) before the **time** (*every day, after school*).

*தமிழ்:* இடம் (எங்கே?) பின்னர் நேரம் (எப்போது?)

**Examples:**

- I study English every morning.
- He plays cricket after school.
- She reads books at home.
- They watch television at night.
- We eat rice for lunch.
- My father reads the newspaper in the garden every morning.
- The students clean the classroom on Fridays.
- Amma buys vegetables at the market on Sundays.
- I meet my friends in the park in the evening.
- We visit our grandparents in Jaffna every April.

### Level 4 — Adding descriptions

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-A04","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"A","level":4,"difficulty":"Beginner"} -->

**Pattern:** ... a/an + adjective + noun ...

**Adjectives** describe nouns. Put them **before** the noun: *a red bicycle*, not *a bicycle red*.

*தமிழ்:* பெயரடை — பெயர்ச்சொல்லுக்கு முன்

**Examples:**

- I have a bicycle.
- I have a red bicycle.
- I have a beautiful red bicycle.
- I ride my bicycle every morning.
- Kandy is a beautiful city.
- My grandmother has long white hair.
- We live in a small house near the river.
- He wears a clean white shirt to school.
- There is a tall coconut tree in our garden.
- She is a kind and helpful teacher.

**Watch the sentence grow:**

1. I have a bicycle.
2. I have a red bicycle.
3. I have a beautiful red bicycle.
4. I ride my beautiful red bicycle to school every morning.

### Level 5 — Adding reasons

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-A05","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"A","level":5,"difficulty":"Beginner"} -->

**Pattern:** ... because + subject + verb ...

Use **because** to give a reason. After *because*, write a full sentence (subject + verb). After *like*, use **verb + -ing**: *I like playing*.

*தமிழ்:* because = ஏனெனில்

**Examples:**

- I like cricket.
- I like playing cricket.
- I like playing cricket because it is enjoyable.
- I like playing cricket with my friends because it makes me happy.
- I drink water because I am thirsty.
- She is happy because she passed the exam.
- We stayed at home because it was raining.
- He wears glasses because he cannot see well.
- I study hard because I want to become a doctor.
- Trees are important because they give us oxygen.

**Watch the sentence grow:**

1. I like cricket.
2. I like playing cricket.
3. I like playing cricket because it is enjoyable.
4. I like playing cricket with my friends because it makes me happy.

### Level 6 — Connecting sentences

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-A06","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"A","level":6,"difficulty":"Beginner"} -->

**Pattern:** Sentence 1 + linking word + Sentence 2

**and** adds, **but** shows a difference, **so** shows a result, **because** gives a reason, **although** shows a surprise. Use a comma before *but* and *so* when joining two full sentences.

*தமிழ்:* and = மற்றும், but = ஆனால், so = எனவே, although = இருந்தபோதிலும்

**Examples:**

- I like cricket, but my brother likes football.
- Although I was tired, I completed my homework.
- It was raining, so we stayed at home.
- She is clever and hard-working.
- I went to the shop and bought some bread.
- He studied hard, so he passed the exam.
- Although the bus was late, we reached school on time.
- My father is a farmer, and my mother is a teacher.
- I called him, but he did not answer.
- We can go by bus or by train.

**Watch the sentence grow:**

1. I like cricket.
2. My brother likes football.
3. I like cricket, but my brother likes football.

**Practice — Level 6:**

### Level 7 — Constructing paragraphs

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-A07","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"A","level":7,"difficulty":"Beginner"} -->

**Pattern:** Topic sentence + 3–4 supporting sentences + concluding sentence

A **paragraph** is a group of sentences about **one** idea. Start with a sentence that tells the topic, add details and reasons, and finish with a short ending.

*தமிழ்:* பந்தி — ஒரு கருத்தைப் பற்றிய வாக்கியங்களின் தொகுப்பு

**Examples:**

> My favourite sport is cricket. I play cricket with my friends after school. Cricket helps me stay healthy. It also teaches me teamwork. I enjoy playing cricket very much.

> My mother is the most important person in my life. She wakes up early every day and cooks for the family. She helps me with my studies. When I am sick, she takes care of me. I love my mother very much.

> Our school library is a quiet and useful place. It has more than five thousand books. Students can read newspapers and magazines there. I borrow a story book every week. The library helps us improve our reading.


**Watch the sentence grow:**

1. My favourite sport is cricket. (topic)
2. I play cricket with my friends after school. (detail)
3. Cricket helps me stay healthy. (reason 1)
4. It also teaches me teamwork. (reason 2)
5. I enjoy playing cricket very much. (ending)

### Different kinds of sentences

#### Positive sentences

Say that something happens or is true.

- I like tea.
- She plays the violin.
- We went to Galle last week.

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-SB-0043","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","type":"sentence_building","difficulty":"Beginner","answer_type":"exact"} -->
**ENG-SB-0043** Make positive: (they / live / in Jaffna)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

#### Negative sentences

Add **not**: *do not / does not / did not + base verb*, or *is not / are not / was not*, *cannot*.

- I do not like tea.
- She does not play the violin.
- We did not go to Galle.
- He is not at home.
- I cannot swim.

#### Questions

Put the helping verb first: *Do/Does/Did/Is/Are/Can + subject + ...?* For information, start with *what, where, when, who, why, how*.

- Do you like tea?
- Does she play the violin?
- Did you go to Galle?
- Where do you live?
- Why is he late?

#### Requests

Ask politely with *please, could you, may I*.

### 1. Basic sentence construction

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0001","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ZERO","section":"B","chapter":1,"related":["ENG-TOP-POS","ENG-TOP-SVA","ENG-TOP-WRIT"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Build a correct sentence with a subject and a verb, then add more information.

Every English sentence needs a **subject** (who or what) and a **verb** (the action or state). Then we can add an object, a place, a time and a reason.

*தமிழ்:* எழுவாய் (subject) + வினை (verb) இல்லாமல் வாக்கியம் இல்லை.

**Important rules**

- A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop (.), a question mark (?) or an exclamation mark (!).
- Normal order: Subject + Verb + Object + Place + Time.
- Do not leave out the verb: ~~My mother teacher.~~ → My mother **is** a teacher.

**Sentence structures**

- S + V: *Birds fly.*
- S + V + O: *Nimal reads books.*
- S + V + O + Place: *Nimal reads books in the library.*
- S + V + O + Place + Time: *Nimal reads books in the library every day.*

**Correct examples**

- ✅ The baby sleeps.
- ✅ My father drives a van.
- ✅ We grow vegetables in our garden.
- ✅ The students clean the classroom every Friday.
- ✅ Kavya is a good singer.
- ✅ It is raining.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Is raining. | It is raining. | English needs a subject. Use *it* for weather. |
| My brother very tall. | My brother is very tall. | A sentence needs a verb. Use *is* before an adjective. |
| Every day I to school go. | I go to school every day. | Use the order Subject + Verb + Place + Time. |
| i like mangoes | I like mangoes. | *I* is always a capital letter; end with a full stop. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Missing *is/am/are* before adjectives and nouns.
- Translating word order directly from Tamil (verb at the end).
- Missing full stops between sentences.

**Practice**

1. Put in order: football / play / we / after school
2. Add a verb: My sister ___ a nurse.
3. Correct it: Very hot today.
4. Add a place: I read books ___.
5. Add a time: They watch television ___.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. We play football after school.
2. is
3. It is very hot today.
4. e.g. I read books at home. / in the library.
5. e.g. They watch television in the evening.

</details>

### 2. Parts of speech

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0002","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-POS","section":"B","chapter":2,"related":["ENG-TOP-WF","ENG-TOP-ADJ","ENG-TOP-ADV"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Recognise the eight word classes and use each one correctly.

Each word in a sentence has a job. Knowing the job helps you choose the correct form.

*தமிழ்:* சொல் வகைகள் — பெயர்ச்சொல், வினைச்சொல், பெயரடை, வினையடை...

**Important rules**

- Noun – name of a person, place, thing or idea (*teacher, Kandy, book, happiness*).
- Pronoun – takes the place of a noun (*he, it, them*).
- Verb – action or state (*run, is, have*).
- Adjective – describes a noun (*tall, red, kind*).
- Adverb – describes a verb, adjective or another adverb (*quickly, very, always*).
- Preposition – shows place, time or direction (*in, on, at, to*).
- Conjunction – joins words or sentences (*and, but, because*).
- Interjection – shows feeling (*Oh! Wow!*).

**Sentence structures**

- The **tall** (adj) **boy** (noun) **runs** (verb) **quickly** (adv) **to** (prep) **school** (noun).

**Correct examples**

- ✅ *Kandy* is a beautiful city. (noun)
- ✅ *She* helps me. (pronoun)
- ✅ The children *laughed*. (verb)
- ✅ It is a *beautiful* garden. (adjective)
- ✅ He speaks *slowly*. (adverb)
- ✅ The cat is *under* the table. (preposition)
- ✅ I was tired, *but* I finished my work. (conjunction)

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| She sings beautiful. | She sings beautifully. | To describe a verb (*sings*), use an adverb. |
| He is a carefully driver. | He is a careful driver. | To describe a noun (*driver*), use an adjective. |
| The happy of the children was clear. | The happiness of the children was clear. | After *the* we need a noun: *happiness*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Using an adjective where an adverb is needed (*He runs quick*).
- Confusing noun and verb forms in word-formation tests (*advice/advise*).

**Practice**

1. What part of speech is *quickly*?
2. What part of speech is *under*?
3. Choose: He answered ___ . (correct/correctly)
4. Name the noun: The girl is singing.
5. Name the adjective: We saw a huge ship.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. Adverb
2. Preposition
3. correctly
4. girl
5. huge

</details>

### 3. Nouns

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0003","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-NOUN","section":"B","chapter":3,"related":["ENG-TOP-ART","ENG-TOP-SVA"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Use singular, plural, countable and uncountable nouns correctly.

Countable nouns can be counted (*one book, two books*). Uncountable nouns cannot (*water, advice, information*).

*தமிழ்:* எண்ணக்கூடிய / எண்ண முடியாத பெயர்ச்சொற்கள்

**Important rules**

- Most plurals add *-s*: book → books.
- Add *-es* after s, x, ch, sh, o (some): box → boxes, watch → watches, potato → potatoes.
- Consonant + y → -ies: baby → babies; vowel + y → -s: toy → toys.
- Some plurals are irregular: child → children, man → men, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice.
- Uncountable nouns have no plural: ~~informations~~, ~~advices~~, ~~furnitures~~.

**Sentence structures**

- a/an + singular countable noun
- many + plural countable noun
- much + uncountable noun
- some/a lot of + both

**Correct examples**

- ✅ There are three boxes on the table.
- ✅ The children are playing.
- ✅ She gave me some good advice.
- ✅ We need much water in the dry season.
- ✅ How many students are there in your class?
- ✅ Two women are waiting at the bus stop.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I have two childs. | I have two children. | *Child* has an irregular plural. |
| She gave me many advices. | She gave me a lot of advice. | *Advice* is uncountable. |
| There are many furnitures in the room. | There is a lot of furniture in the room. | *Furniture* is uncountable. |
| I bought three tomatos. | I bought three tomatoes. | *Tomato* adds -es. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Adding -s to uncountable nouns (*informations, homeworks*).
- Forgetting irregular plurals in cloze tests.

**Practice**

1. Plural of *knife*?
2. Plural of *city*?
3. many or much: How ___ sugar do you need?
4. Correct: I have many homeworks.
5. Plural of *sheep*?

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. knives
2. cities
3. much
4. I have a lot of homework.
5. sheep

</details>

### 4. Pronouns

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0004","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-PRON","section":"B","chapter":4,"related":["ENG-TOP-NOUN","ENG-TOP-QTAG"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Use subject, object and possessive pronouns correctly.

Pronouns stop us repeating nouns. Choose the form by its job in the sentence.

*தமிழ்:* பிரதிப்பெயர்கள் — he (அவன்), she (அவள்), they (அவர்கள்)

**Important rules**

- Subject: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.
- Object: me, you, him, her, it, us, them.
- Possessive adjective: my, your, his, her, its, our, their (+ noun).
- Possessive pronoun: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs (no noun after).
- Reflexive: myself, yourself, himself, herself, ourselves, themselves.

**Sentence structures**

- Subject pronoun + verb: *She plays.*
- Verb + object pronoun: *Help me.*
- Possessive + noun: *This is my bag.*

**Correct examples**

- ✅ Nimal is my friend. He lives near my house.
- ✅ The teacher praised us.
- ✅ This pen is mine.
- ✅ The dog wagged its tail.
- ✅ They did the project themselves.
- ✅ Kavya and I went to the market.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Me and Nimal went to the shop. | Nimal and I went to the shop. | Use the subject pronoun *I* before a verb; put the other person first. |
| This book is her. | This book is hers. | Use the possessive pronoun *hers* without a noun. |
| The cat drank it's milk. | The cat drank its milk. | *Its* = possessive; *it's* = it is. |
| Give it to she. | Give it to her. | After a preposition use an object pronoun. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Mixing *its* and *it's*.
- Using *he/she* after prepositions.

**Practice**

1. Choose: (He/Him) is my cousin.
2. Choose: Please help (we/us).
3. Choose: That bag is (my/mine).
4. Complete: She cut ___ with a knife. (reflexive)
5. Correct: Me and my sister like music.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. He
2. us
3. mine
4. herself
5. My sister and I like music.

### 5. Articles and determiners

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0005","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ART","section":"B","chapter":5,"related":["ENG-TOP-NOUN"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Choose a, an, the or no article.

*A/an* = one, not specific. *The* = a specific thing we both know. Use *an* before a vowel **sound**.

*தமிழ்:* a / an — ஒன்று; the — குறிப்பிட்ட ஒன்று

**Important rules**

- *a* + consonant sound: a book, a university (/ju/), a uniform.
- *an* + vowel sound: an apple, an hour (silent h), an honest man.
- *the* for something already mentioned or unique: the sun, the moon, the Prime Minister.
- No article before general plural/uncountable nouns: *Dogs are loyal.* *Water is important.*
- Use *the* with rivers, oceans and mountain ranges: the Mahaweli, the Indian Ocean.
- No article before most countries, cities and meals: Sri Lanka, Kandy, breakfast.

**Sentence structures**

- a/an + singular countable noun
- the + specific noun
- this/that + singular, these/those + plural

**Correct examples**

- ✅ I saw an elephant at the zoo. The elephant was very big.
- ✅ She is an engineer.
- ✅ We go to school by bus.
- ✅ The sun rises in the east.
- ✅ My father reads the newspaper every morning.
- ✅ I need a pen.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| She is a honest girl. | She is an honest girl. | *Honest* starts with a vowel sound (the h is silent). |
| He studies at an university. | He studies at a university. | *University* starts with a /j/ sound. |
| The life is beautiful. | Life is beautiful. | No article for general uncountable ideas. |
| I saw moon last night. | I saw the moon last night. | There is only one moon — use *the*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Choosing a/an by spelling instead of sound.
- Adding *the* before general ideas (*the nature, the education*).

**Practice**

1. ___ hour
2. ___ European country
3. I bought a bag. ___ bag is blue.
4. ___ Mahaweli is a long river.
5. Correct: He is a engineer.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. an
2. a
3. The
4. The
5. He is an engineer.

</details>

**Sentence structures**

- am/is/are + verb-ing

- have/has + past participle

**Correct examples**

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0007","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-ADJ","section":"B","chapter":7,"related":["ENG-TOP-COMP","ENG-TOP-POS"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Use adjectives in the correct position and form.

Adjectives describe nouns. In English they come **before** the noun or **after** *be/look/seem*.

### 9. Tenses

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0009","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-TENSE","section":"B","chapter":9,"related":["ENG-TOP-VERB","ENG-TOP-SVA"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Choose the correct tense from time words and meaning.

The tense shows **when** something happens. Time words help you choose.

*தமிழ்:* காலம் — நிகழ்காலம், இறந்தகாலம், எதிர்காலம்

**Important rules**

- Present simple – habits and facts: *every day, always, usually*. (He **plays**.)
- Present continuous – happening now: *now, at the moment*. (He **is playing**.)
- Past simple – finished past: *yesterday, last week, ago, in 2020*. (He **played**.)
- Past continuous – in progress in the past: *at 8 p.m. yesterday, when…*. (He **was playing**.)
- Present perfect – past with present result: *already, just, yet, since, for, ever*. (He **has played**.)
- Future simple – *tomorrow, next week*. (He **will play**.)
- Past perfect – the earlier of two past actions. (*The train **had left** when we arrived.*)

**Sentence structures**

- Present simple: S + V(s)
- Present continuous: S + am/is/are + V-ing
- Past simple: S + V2
- Past continuous: S + was/were + V-ing
- Present perfect: S + have/has + V3
- Future: S + will + V

**Correct examples**

- ✅ My mother cooks rice every day.
- ✅ Look! The children are playing in the rain.
- ✅ We visited Sigiriya last year.
- ✅ I was sleeping when the phone rang.
- ✅ She has just finished her homework.
- ✅ They will go to Jaffna next week.
- ✅ When I reached the station, the train had left.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Yesterday I go to town. | Yesterday I went to town. | *Yesterday* needs the past simple. |
| She is going to school every day. | She goes to school every day. | *Every day* = habit → present simple. |
| I have seen him yesterday. | I saw him yesterday. | Do not use present perfect with a finished time like *yesterday*. |
| He will goes tomorrow. | He will go tomorrow. | After *will*, use the base verb. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Mixing tenses inside one paragraph or story.
- Present perfect with *yesterday/last*.
- Forgetting *-s* in the present simple.

**Practice**

1. She ___ (visit) her aunt last Sunday.
2. Listen! Someone ___ (knock) at the door.
3. I ___ (live) here since 2019.
4. They ___ (travel) to Galle tomorrow.
5. While we ___ (walk), it started to rain.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. visited
2. is knocking
3. have lived
4. will travel
5. were walking

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0010"></a>
### 10. Subject–verb agreement

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0010","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-SVA","section":"B","chapter":10,"related":["ENG-TOP-TENSE","ENG-TOP-NOUN"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Make the verb agree with its subject.

In the present simple, a singular third-person subject (*he, she, it, Nimal, my mother*) takes **-s/-es** on the verb.

*தமிழ்:* he/she/it உடன் வினைக்கு -s சேர்க்கவும்

**Important rules**

- He/She/It + verb-s: *She goes.*
- I/You/We/They + base verb: *They go.*
- *is/was* with singular, *are/were* with plural.
- *There is* + singular, *There are* + plural.
- *Each/every/everyone* + singular verb: *Everyone is here.*
- Two subjects joined by *and* → plural: *Nimal and Kavya are friends.*

**Sentence structures**

- singular subject + verb-s / is / was / has
- plural subject + base verb / are / were / have

**Correct examples**

- ✅ He plays cricket.
- ✅ She goes to school.
- ✅ They play cricket.
- ✅ We go to school.
- ✅ My mother has a sewing machine.
- ✅ There are five students in the room.
- ✅ Everyone likes the new teacher.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| He play cricket. | He plays cricket. | *He* is third-person singular — add *-s*. |
| She go to school. | She goes to school. | *Go* becomes *goes* with *she*. |
| There is five students. | There are five students. | *Five students* is plural. |
| She have a bicycle. | She has a bicycle. | Use *has* with *she*. |
| The boys was late. | The boys were late. | Plural subject → *were*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Forgetting *-s* after *he/she/it* — the most common O/L error.
- Agreeing with the nearest noun: ~~The box of pencils are~~ → The box of pencils **is**.

**Practice**

1. My father ___ (work) in a bank.
2. The children ___ (be) happy.
3. There ___ (be) a cat on the wall.
4. Everyone ___ (want) to win.
5. Nimal and Ravi ___ (live) in Matara.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. works
2. are
3. is
4. wants
5. live

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0011"></a>
### 11. Prepositions

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0011","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-PREP","section":"B","chapter":11,"related":["ENG-TOP-VOC"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Use prepositions of time, place and movement.

Prepositions show the relationship of time, place or direction. Learn them in phrases.

*தமிழ்:* இடைச்சொற்கள் — in, on, at

**Important rules**

- Time: *at* + clock time (at 7 o'clock), *on* + day/date (on Monday, on 5 May), *in* + month/year/season (in May, in 2025).
- Place: *at* a point (at the bus stop), *in* inside (in the box), *on* a surface (on the table).
- Movement: *to* (go to school), *into* (walk into the room), *from* (come from Kandy), *across*, *along*, *through*.
- Fixed phrases: good **at**, interested **in**, afraid **of**, depend **on**, listen **to**, wait **for**.

**Sentence structures**

- at + time
- on + day/date
- in + month/year
- adjective/verb + fixed preposition

**Correct examples**

- ✅ The class starts at 7.30 a.m.
- ✅ We have a test on Friday.
- ✅ My birthday is in June.
- ✅ The book is on the shelf.
- ✅ She is good at mathematics.
- ✅ He walked into the classroom.
- ✅ We are waiting for the bus.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I was born on 2010. | I was born in 2010. | Use *in* with years. |
| She is good in drawing. | She is good at drawing. | Fixed phrase: *good at*. |
| We reached to Kandy at noon. | We reached Kandy at noon. | *Reach* takes no preposition. |
| He is married with a doctor. | He is married to a doctor. | Fixed phrase: *married to*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- *in/on/at* for time.
- Adding *to* after *reach* and *enter*.

**Practice**

1. ___ Monday
2. ___ 6 o'clock
3. ___ December
4. She is afraid ___ dogs.
5. Listen ___ the teacher.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. on
2. at
3. in
4. of
5. to

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0012"></a>
### 12. Conjunctions and linking words

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0012","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-CONJ","section":"B","chapter":12,"related":["ENG-TOP-WRIT","ENG-TOP-REL"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Join ideas with suitable conjunctions and linkers.

Conjunctions join words and sentences. Choose the one that shows the right relationship.

*தமிழ்:* இணைப்புச் சொற்கள் — and, but, because, so

**Important rules**

- Add: *and, also, in addition*.
- Contrast: *but, however, although*.
- Reason: *because, since, as*.
- Result: *so, therefore*.
- Time: *when, while, before, after*.
- Do not use *although* and *but* together: ~~Although it rained, but we played.~~

**Sentence structures**

- Sentence + , but + sentence
- Although + clause, main clause
- Sentence. However, sentence.

**Correct examples**

- ✅ I like cricket, but my brother likes football.
- ✅ Although I was tired, I completed my homework.
- ✅ It was raining, so we stayed at home.
- ✅ She went to bed early because she was sick.
- ✅ Exercise is good for health. Therefore, we should exercise daily.
- ✅ While I was reading, my sister was cooking.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Although it was raining, but we went out. | Although it was raining, we went out. | Use only one contrast word. |
| Because I was sick. I stayed at home. | I stayed at home because I was sick. | A *because*-clause cannot stand alone. |
| He is poor but he is honest and. | He is poor, but he is honest. | Do not end a sentence with *and*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Double conjunctions (*although…but*).
- Sentence fragments starting with *because*.

**Practice**

1. Join with *but*: I called him. He did not answer.
2. Join with *because*: We stayed inside. It was raining.
3. Join with *although*: She was ill. She came to school.
4. Choose: He studied hard, (so/but) he passed.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. I called him, but he did not answer.
2. We stayed inside because it was raining.
3. Although she was ill, she came to school.
4. so

</details>

### 13. Modal verbs

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0013","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-MODAL","section":"B","chapter":13,"related":["ENG-TOP-VERB","ENG-TOP-DIAL"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Use can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would.

Modal verbs add meaning like ability, permission, advice or obligation. Always use the **base verb** after a modal.

*தமிழ்:* Modal verbs — can (முடியும்), must (வேண்டும்), should (வேண்டும்/நல்லது)

**Important rules**

- can/could – ability, requests: *I can swim.* *Could you help me?*
- may/might – possibility, permission: *It may rain.* *May I come in?*
- must/have to – necessity: *You must wear a helmet.*
- should – advice: *You should drink more water.*
- Modal + base verb (no -s, no to): ~~He can swims~~, ~~She must to go~~.

**Sentence structures**

- Subject + modal + base verb
- Modal + not + base verb
- Modal + subject + base verb + ? (question)

**Correct examples**

- ✅ My sister can play the violin.
- ✅ You should see a doctor.
- ✅ Students must wear the school uniform.
- ✅ It might rain this afternoon.
- ✅ May I use your pen?
- ✅ Could you open the window, please?

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| He can swims well. | He can swim well. | Use the base verb after *can*. |
| You must to obey the rules. | You must obey the rules. | No *to* after *must*. |
| She cans speak French. | She can speak French. | Modals never take *-s*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Adding *to* or *-s* after modals.
- Using *must* for polite requests (use *could/would*).

**Practice**

1. Advice: You ___ eat more vegetables.
2. Permission: ___ I leave early?
3. Correct: He must goes now.
4. Ability (past): When I was five, I ___ ride a bicycle.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. should
2. May / Can
3. He must go now.
4. could

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0014"></a>
### 14. Active and passive voice

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0014","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-PASS","section":"B","chapter":14,"related":["ENG-TOP-TENSE","ENG-TOP-VERB"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Change active sentences into the passive and use the passive in writing.

In the passive, the object of the action becomes the subject. Use it when the action is more important than the doer.

*தமிழ்:* செயப்பாட்டுவினை — be + past participle

**Important rules**

- Passive = be (in the correct tense) + past participle.
- Present: is/are + V3 (*English is spoken here.*)
- Past: was/were + V3 (*The bridge was built in 1990.*)
- Future: will be + V3; Perfect: has/have been + V3; Modal: can/must be + V3.
- Add *by* + doer only if it is important.

**Sentence structures**

- Active: S + V + O → Passive: O + be + V3 (+ by S)

**Correct examples**

- ✅ Rice is grown in many parts of Sri Lanka.
- ✅ The windows were cleaned yesterday.
- ✅ The prizes will be given by the principal.
- ✅ The match has been cancelled.
- ✅ Mobile phones must be switched off.
- ✅ The letter was written by Kavya.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The house was build in 2010. | The house was built in 2010. | Use the past participle *built*. |
| English speak in many countries. | English is spoken in many countries. | Passive needs *be*. |
| The cakes was made by my mother. | The cakes were made by my mother. | *Cakes* is plural → *were*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Wrong form of *be* for plural subjects.
- Using the past simple instead of the past participle.

**Practice**

1. Passive: They grow tea in Nuwara Eliya.
2. Passive: The teacher marked the papers.
3. Passive: They will open the new library tomorrow.
4. Complete: The thief ___ (catch) last night.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. Tea is grown in Nuwara Eliya.
2. The papers were marked by the teacher.
3. The new library will be opened tomorrow.
4. was caught

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0015"></a>
### 15. Direct and indirect speech

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0015","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-REP","section":"B","chapter":15,"related":["ENG-TOP-TENSE","ENG-TOP-PUNC"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Report what someone said.

Direct speech gives the exact words in quotation marks. Indirect (reported) speech tells the meaning, usually one tense back.

*தமிழ்:* நேர்கூற்று / அயற்கூற்று

**Important rules**

- present simple → past simple; present continuous → past continuous; past simple/present perfect → past perfect; will → would; can → could.
- Change pronouns and time words: *today → that day*, *tomorrow → the next day*, *here → there*, *this → that*.
- Questions: *He asked me where I lived.* (statement word order; no question mark).
- Commands: *She told me to close the door.* / *She told me not to talk.*

**Sentence structures**

- said (that) + clause
- asked + if/whether / wh-word + clause
- told + object + to + verb

**Correct examples**

- ✅ Kavya said, "I am tired." → Kavya said that she was tired.
- ✅ He said, "I will come tomorrow." → He said that he would come the next day.
- ✅ The teacher said, "Open your books." → The teacher told us to open our books.
- ✅ Nimal asked, "Where do you live?" → Nimal asked me where I lived.
- ✅ Mother asked, "Have you eaten?" → Mother asked if I had eaten.
- ✅ She said, "I can swim." → She said that she could swim.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| He asked me where do I live. | He asked me where I lived. | Use statement order in reported questions. |
| She told that she was busy. | She said that she was busy. | *Told* needs an object: *told me*; otherwise use *said*. |
| The teacher said us to sit down. | The teacher told us to sit down. | Use *told + object + to* for commands. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Keeping question word order.
- Not changing pronouns.

**Practice**

1. Report: Ravi said, "I like mangoes."
2. Report: She said, "Don't be late."
3. Report: He asked, "Are you ready?"
4. Report: They said, "We are playing."

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. Ravi said that he liked mangoes.
2. She told me not to be late.
3. He asked if I was ready.
4. They said that they were playing.

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0016"></a>
### 16. Question formation

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0016","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-QFORM","section":"B","chapter":16,"related":["ENG-TOP-VERB","ENG-TOP-QTAG"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Make yes/no and wh- questions correctly.

To make a question, put the auxiliary before the subject. Use *do/does/did* when there is no auxiliary.

*தமிழ்:* வினா வாக்கியங்கள் — Where, When, Why, How

**Important rules**

- Yes/No: Aux + S + V? (*Is she coming? Can you swim? Did they win?*)
- Wh-: Wh-word + Aux + S + V? (*Where do you live?*)
- Who/What as subject: no *do* (*Who broke the window?*)
- Wh-words: who (person), what (thing), where (place), when (time), why (reason), how (manner), how many (number), how much (amount), how long (duration), which (choice), whose (owner).

**Sentence structures**

- (Wh-) + do/does/did + S + base verb?
- (Wh-) + am/is/are/was/were + S + …?
- (Wh-) + have/has + S + V3?

**Correct examples**

- ✅ Where do you live?
- ✅ What time does the bus leave?
- ✅ Why were you absent yesterday?
- ✅ How many brothers have you got?
- ✅ Who wrote this letter?
- ✅ Did you watch the match?
- ✅ When will the results be released?

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where you live? | Where do you live? | Add *do* before the subject. |
| What time the train leaves? | What time does the train leave? | Use *does* + base verb. |
| Why you were late? | Why were you late? | Put *were* before the subject. |
| Who did break the cup? | Who broke the cup? | *Who* is the subject — no *did*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Forgetting the auxiliary.
- Wrong word order in exam Test 10 (question formation).

**Practice**

1. Make a question for the answer: *She lives in Galle.* (Where)
2. Answer: *The match starts at 3 p.m.* (What time)
3. Answer: *He came by bus.* (How)
4. Answer: *They have two cats.* (How many)

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. Where does she live?
2. What time does the match start?
3. How did he come?
4. How many cats do they have?

</details>

### 17. Question tags

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0017","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-QTAG","section":"B","chapter":17,"related":["ENG-TOP-QFORM"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Add correct question tags.

A question tag is a short question at the end of a statement. Positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag.

**Important rules**

- Use the same auxiliary: *She is late, isn't she?*
- No auxiliary → do/does/did: *He plays well, doesn't he?*
- Use a pronoun in the tag: *Nimal is here, isn't he?*
- Special: *I am right, aren't I?* *Let's go, shall we?*

**Sentence structures**

- Positive statement, + negative aux + pronoun?
- Negative statement, + positive aux + pronoun?

**Correct examples**

- ✅ You are a student, aren't you?
- ✅ She can sing, can't she?
- ✅ They didn't come, did they?
- ✅ He has finished, hasn't he?
- ✅ It was cold, wasn't it?
- ✅ Your brother works in Colombo, doesn't he?

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| He plays cricket, isn't he? | He plays cricket, doesn't he? | No auxiliary → use *does*. |
| You can swim, can you? | You can swim, can't you? | Positive statement → negative tag. |
| Kavya is clever, isn't Kavya? | Kavya is clever, isn't she? | Use a pronoun in the tag. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Using *isn't it* for every sentence.
- Not matching the tense.

**Practice**

1. She is your sister, ___?
2. They went home, ___?
3. You won't forget, ___?
4. He has a car, ___?

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. isn't she
2. didn't they
3. will you
4. doesn't he

</details>

### 18. Conditional sentences

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0018","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-COND","section":"B","chapter":18,"related":["ENG-TOP-TENSE"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Make zero, first and second conditional sentences.

Conditionals talk about results that depend on a condition (*if*).

*தமிழ்:* நிபந்தனை வாக்கியங்கள் — If

**Important rules**

- Zero (facts): If + present, present. *If you heat ice, it melts.*
- First (real future): If + present, will + verb. *If it rains, we will stay inside.*
- Second (unreal/imaginary): If + past, would + verb. *If I had wings, I would fly.*
- Do not use *will* in the *if*-clause: ~~If it will rain~~.
- *Unless* = if … not.

**Sentence structures**

- If + present simple, will + base verb
- If + past simple, would + base verb

**Correct examples**

- ✅ If you mix red and white, you get pink.
- ✅ If you study hard, you will pass the exam.
- ✅ If I see Nimal, I will tell him.
- ✅ If I were rich, I would build a library.
- ✅ Unless you hurry, you will miss the bus.
- ✅ We will go to the beach if the weather is good.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| If it will rain, we will cancel the trip. | If it rains, we will cancel the trip. | Use the present simple after *if*. |
| If I would be you, I would apologise. | If I were you, I would apologise. | Use *were* in the *if*-clause. |
| If he come, I will tell him. | If he comes, I will tell him. | *He* + comes. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- *will* in the if-clause.
- Mixing first and second conditionals.

**Practice**

1. If you ___ (water) the plants, they will grow.
2. If I ___ (have) a car, I would drive to Jaffna.
3. If it rains, the match ___ (be) cancelled.
4. Unless you ___ (study), you will fail.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. water
2. had
3. will be
4. study

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0019"></a>
### 19. Comparative and superlative forms

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0019","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-COMP","section":"B","chapter":19,"related":["ENG-TOP-ADJ","ENG-TOP-CHART"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Compare two or more things correctly.

Use the comparative for two things and the superlative for three or more.

*தமிழ்:* ஒப்பீடு — -er/more, -est/most

**Important rules**

- Short adjectives: -er / -est (tall, taller, tallest).
- Consonant-vowel-consonant: double the consonant (big → bigger).
- -y → -ier / -iest (happy → happier).
- Long adjectives: more / most (beautiful → more beautiful).
- Irregular: good → better → best; bad → worse → worst; far → farther/further.
- Comparative + *than*; *the* + superlative.
- Never use *more* + -er: ~~more taller~~.

**Sentence structures**

- A is + comparative + than B
- A is the + superlative + in/of …
- A is as + adjective + as B

**Correct examples**

- ✅ My brother is taller than me.
- ✅ This test is easier than the last one.
- ✅ Sigiriya is one of the most beautiful places in Sri Lanka.
- ✅ Nimal is the best player in the team.
- ✅ A bus is not as fast as a train.
- ✅ This is the biggest classroom in the school.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| She is more taller than me. | She is taller than me. | Do not use *more* with *-er*. |
| He is the most tallest boy. | He is the tallest boy. | Do not use *most* with *-est*. |
| This is gooder than that. | This is better than that. | *Good* is irregular. |
| Kandy is more cooler than Colombo. | Kandy is cooler than Colombo. | One comparison marker only. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Double comparatives (*more taller*).
- Forgetting *the* before superlatives.

**Practice**

1. Comparative of *heavy*?
2. Superlative of *bad*?
3. A car is ___ (expensive) than a bicycle.
4. He is the ___ (fast) runner in the class.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. heavier
2. worst
3. more expensive
4. fastest

</details>

### 20. Relative clauses

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0020","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-REL","section":"B","chapter":20,"related":["ENG-TOP-CONJ"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Join sentences with who, which, that, whose and where.

A relative clause gives more information about a noun.

**Important rules**

- *who* for people, *which* for things/animals, *that* for both (in defining clauses).
- *whose* for possession, *where* for places.
- Do not repeat the pronoun: ~~The boy who he won~~ → The boy who won.

**Sentence structures**

- noun + who/which/that + verb …

**Correct examples**

- ✅ The girl who won the prize is my cousin.
- ✅ This is the book which I bought yesterday.
- ✅ The man whose car was stolen called the police.
- ✅ This is the school where my father studied.
- ✅ I have a friend who lives in Jaffna.
- ✅ The phone that I lost was new.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The man which lives next door is a doctor. | The man who lives next door is a doctor. | Use *who* for people. |
| The boy who he won the race is my friend. | The boy who won the race is my friend. | Do not repeat the subject. |
| That is the house where I was born there. | That is the house where I was born. | *Where* already means *in that place*. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Repeating pronouns after *who/which*.

**Practice**

1. Join: I met a girl. She can speak four languages.
2. Join: This is the bag. I bought it in Kandy.
3. Join: I know a boy. His father is a pilot.
4. Join: This is the hospital. I was born here.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. I met a girl who can speak four languages.
2. This is the bag which/that I bought in Kandy.
3. I know a boy whose father is a pilot.
4. This is the hospital where I was born.

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0021"></a>
### 21. Gerunds and infinitives

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0021","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-GER","section":"B","chapter":21,"related":["ENG-TOP-VERB","ENG-TOP-PREP"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Use verb-ing and to + verb after the right words.

Some verbs are followed by *-ing*; others by *to + verb*.

**Important rules**

- + -ing: enjoy, finish, avoid, mind, practise, keep, stop (stop doing), suggest; after prepositions (*good at swimming, interested in reading*).
- + to: want, decide, hope, plan, agree, need, learn, promise, would like.
- Both: like, love, start, begin (*I like reading / I like to read*).
- -ing as a subject: *Swimming is good exercise.*

**Sentence structures**

- verb + verb-ing
- verb + to + base verb
- preposition + verb-ing

**Correct examples**

- ✅ I enjoy playing badminton.
- ✅ She decided to join the drama club.
- ✅ He is good at drawing.
- ✅ We hope to visit Jaffna.
- ✅ Reading improves your vocabulary.
- ✅ Thank you for helping me.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I like play cricket. | I like playing cricket. / I like to play cricket. | After *like*, use *-ing* or *to + verb*. |
| She enjoys to dance. | She enjoys dancing. | *Enjoy* + -ing. |
| I am interested to learn French. | I am interested in learning French. | After a preposition, use *-ing*. |
| He wants going home. | He wants to go home. | *Want* + to. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- *like play*, *enjoy to*.
- Bare verb after a preposition.

**Practice**

1. I finished ___ (write) the essay.
2. They agreed ___ (help) us.
3. She is afraid of ___ (swim).
4. We would like ___ (thank) you.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. writing
2. to help
3. swimming
4. to thank

</details>

**Related topics:** `ENG-TOP-VERB` (Verbs and auxiliary verbs), `ENG-TOP-PREP` (Prepositions)

### 22. Word formation

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0022","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-WF","section":"B","chapter":22,"related":["ENG-TOP-POS","ENG-TOP-VOC"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Change a word into the noun, verb, adjective or adverb needed by the sentence.

Look at the gap: after *a/the* you need a noun; before a noun an adjective; after a verb an adverb.

*தமிழ்:* சொல்லாக்கம் — பின்னொட்டு, முன்னொட்டு

**Important rules**

- Noun suffixes: -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -er/-or, -ance.
- Adjective suffixes: -ful, -less, -ous, -ive, -able, -al.
- Adverb: adjective + -ly.
- Verb suffixes: -ise, -en, -ify.
- Negative prefixes: un-, in-, im-, dis-, ir-.

**Sentence structures**

- the + NOUN
- a/an + ADJECTIVE + noun
- verb + ADVERB

**Correct examples**

- ✅ Education is important. (educate → education)
- ✅ She answered correctly. (correct → correctly)
- ✅ The film was enjoyable. (enjoy → enjoyable)
- ✅ We need more information. (inform → information)
- ✅ His behaviour was unacceptable. (accept → unacceptable)
- ✅ The flood caused great destruction. (destroy → destruction)

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Health is very importance. | Health is very important. | After *very* use an adjective. |
| She gave a beautifully speech. | She gave a beautiful speech. | Before a noun use an adjective. |
| We must protection the environment. | We must protect the environment. | After *must* use a verb. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Choosing the wrong word class for the gap.
- Spelling suffixes (*beautifull, happyness*).

**Practice**

1. We should take ___ (care) when crossing roads.
2. The team played ___ (success).
3. Smoking is ___ (harm) to health.
4. Thank you for your ___ (kind).

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. care
2. successfully
3. harmful
4. kindness

</details>

### 23. Vocabulary development

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0023","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-VOC","section":"B","chapter":23,"related":["ENG-TOP-WF","ENG-TOP-CONF","ENG-TOP-PHRV"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Learn words in topics, families and collocations.

Learn words with their partners and examples, not alone. Use new words in your own sentences.

**Important rules**

- Learn word families: protect – protection – protective.
- Learn collocations: *make a decision*, *do homework*, *take a photo*.
- Guess meaning from context: look at the words around the new word.
- Keep a personal word notebook with Tamil meaning and an example.

**Sentence structures**

- word + meaning + example + related words

**Correct examples**

- ✅ We must **protect** the environment.
- ✅ She **made a decision** to study medicine.
- ✅ I **do my homework** after dinner.
- ✅ The flood **damaged** many houses.
- ✅ He **looks after** his grandmother.
- ✅ The **crowded** bus moved slowly.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I did a mistake. | I made a mistake. | Collocation: *make a mistake*. |
| She said me the answer. | She told me the answer. | *Tell* + person. |
| He lent my book. | He borrowed my book. | *Borrow* = take; *lend* = give. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Confusing *make/do*, *say/tell*, *borrow/lend*.

**Practice**

1. ___ a photo (make/take)
2. ___ homework (do/make)
3. Opposite of *ancient*?
4. Noun of *decide*?

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. take
2. do
3. modern
4. decision

</details>

<a id="eng-note-0024"></a>
### 24. Punctuation and capitalisation

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0024","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-PUNC","section":"B","chapter":24,"related":["ENG-TOP-WRIT","ENG-TOP-REP"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Use capital letters and punctuation marks correctly.

Punctuation helps the reader. Missing full stops and capitals lose marks in Mechanics.

**Important rules**

- Capital letters: first word of a sentence, *I*, names of people, places, days, months, languages, festivals (*Monday, May, Tamil, Thai Pongal*).
- Full stop (.) at the end of a statement; question mark (?) after a direct question.
- Comma (,) in lists and after linkers: *However, …*
- Apostrophe (') for possession (*Nimal's bag*, *the students' room*) and short forms (*don't*).
- Quotation marks (" ") for direct speech.

**Sentence structures**

- Capital + … + .
- Name's + noun

**Correct examples**

- ✅ My name is Kavya and I live in Jaffna.
- ✅ We bought apples, oranges, bananas and grapes.
- ✅ Where are you going?
- ✅ This is my brother's bicycle.
- ✅ "Come here," said the teacher.
- ✅ However, the match was cancelled.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| my friend lives in colombo | My friend lives in Colombo. | Capital for the first word and for place names; end with a full stop. |
| i dont like it | I don't like it. | *I* is capital; apostrophe in *don't*. |
| Where are you going. | Where are you going? | Questions need a question mark. |
| We visited kandy on monday | We visited Kandy on Monday. | Places and days need capitals. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- No capitals for names, days and months.
- Run-on sentences without full stops.

**Practice**

1. Punctuate: nimal and i went to galle on sunday
2. Punctuate: what is your name
3. Apostrophe: the book of my sister
4. Punctuate: we need rice sugar milk and eggs

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. Nimal and I went to Galle on Sunday.
2. What is your name?
3. my sister's book
4. We need rice, sugar, milk and eggs.

</details>

### 25. Reading comprehension techniques

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0025","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-READ","section":"B","chapter":25,"related":["ENG-TOP-VOC"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Find answers quickly and accurately in a text.

Read the questions first, find the key words, then scan the text for those words.

*தமிழ்:* வாசிப்பு — முதலில் வினாக்களை வாசிக்கவும்

**Important rules**

- Skim: read quickly for the main idea (title, first and last sentences).
- Scan: look quickly for a specific word, number, name or date.
- Answer only what is asked — do not copy whole paragraphs.
- Use context to guess new words.
- For *true/false*, find the exact sentence that proves your answer.
- For pronoun questions, look **back** to the nearest suitable noun.

**Sentence structures**

- Question key word → find it in the text → read the sentence before and after → write a short answer

**Correct examples**

- ✅ Question: *When did the festival start?* Key word: *festival, start* → find the date.
- ✅ Question: *What does 'they' refer to?* → look at the sentence before.
- ✅ Question: *Find a word that means 'very big'.* → look for an adjective in the paragraph.
- ✅ Question: *Why did Kamal go home?* → look for *because / so*.
- ✅ Question: *True or false: The shop opens on Sundays.* → find the opening days.
- ✅ Question: *What is the purpose of this notice?* → read the heading and the request.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (copying a whole paragraph as the answer) | Write only the needed information in a short phrase or sentence. | Copying wastes time and may include wrong information. |
| Answering from your own knowledge. | Answer from the text. | Marks are given only for information in the passage. |
| Guessing true/false without evidence. | Find the line that proves it. | Evidence makes your answer correct. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Not reading the instruction (*one word only*, *tick*, *underline*).
- Long copied answers.

**Practice**

1. What is *scanning*?
2. What should you read first?
3. Where do you find what a pronoun refers to?
4. Should you copy full paragraphs?

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. Looking quickly for a specific piece of information.
2. The questions.
3. Usually in the sentence before.
4. No — write only the needed information.

</details>

**Related topics:** `ENG-TOP-VOC` (Vocabulary development)

<a id="eng-note-0026"></a>
### 26. Introduction to writing skills

<!-- meta {"id":"ENG-NOTE-0026","topic_id":"ENG-TOP-WRIT","section":"B","chapter":26,"related":["ENG-TOP-PARA","ENG-TOP-LETTER","ENG-TOP-ESSAY"]} -->

**Learning objective:** Plan and write short and long answers with correct format.

Good writing = answer the question + cover all points + correct sentences + correct format + correct length.

*தமிழ்:* எழுதுவதற்கு முன் திட்டமிடவும்

**Important rules**

- Underline the key words and content points in the question.
- Plan: list your points before writing.
- Use simple, correct sentences — accuracy is more important than difficult words.
- Use the correct format (notice, letter, speech, essay).
- Keep to the word count (Test 6 about 40–50 words, Test 8 about 50–60, Test 14 about 100, Test 16 about 200 — 2020 official paper).
- Check your work: tense, -s, articles, spelling, punctuation.

**Sentence structures**

- Plan → Write → Check

**Correct examples**

- ✅ Question: *Write a notice about a blood donation camp.* → Points: what, when, where, who, contact.
- ✅ Simple and correct: *Trees give us oxygen. We must protect them.*
- ✅ Linking: *Firstly, … In addition, … Finally, …*
- ✅ Format: a letter has an address, date, salutation, body and ending.
- ✅ Checking: *She go* → *She goes*.
- ✅ Length: count the words in one line and multiply by the number of lines.

**Incorrect → correct**

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Writing about a different topic. | Answer the exact question. | Off-topic answers get 0 for content, which means 0 for the whole answer (2020 marking scheme note). |
| Missing content points. | Tick every point in the question. | Content marks depend on the points. |
| Very long, complicated sentences with many errors. | Short, correct sentences. | Language marks are for accuracy. |

**Common examination mistakes**

- Ignoring the word count.
- Missing format parts (date, salutation, signature).

**Practice**

1. What should you do before writing?
2. Which is better: a simple correct sentence or a difficult wrong one?
3. About how many words for Test 16 (2020)?
4. Name two things to check.

<details><summary>Answers and explanations</summary>

1. Plan your points.
2. A simple correct sentence.
3. About 200 words.
4. e.g. tenses and spelling (also -s, articles, punctuation).

</details>
