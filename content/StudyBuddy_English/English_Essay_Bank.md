# StudyBuddy — English Essay Bank (Extended Writing)

> Essays, articles, speeches, stories and dialogues for **Paper II Test 16** (2020: about 200 words; Content 5 + Language 5 + Format & organisation 2 + Mechanics 3 = 15 marks).
> Topics are practice topics. **They are not predictions of examination questions.**

## Section A — Universal construction

| Paragraph | Purpose | Sentence starters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | introduce the topic | *Today, … has become an important part of our lives.* · *Many people enjoy …* |
| 2. Main ideas | the most important points | *Firstly, …* · *One important reason is …* |
| 3. Supporting ideas | reasons, examples, advantages/disadvantages | *For example, …* · *In addition, …* · *On the other hand, …* |
| 4. Conclusion | summary or final message | *In conclusion, …* · *Therefore, we should …* · *I hope that …* |

Adjust the structure to the task: a **speech** starts with a greeting and ends with *Thank you*; an **article** has a title (and often the writer's name); a **story** follows time order (*One day … Suddenly … Finally …*) and uses past tenses; a **dialogue** uses names and turns.

## Section B — From idea to sentence to paragraph

Idea: *keeps us healthy* → Sentence: *Sports keep us healthy.* → Expanded: *Playing sports regularly keeps our bodies healthy and active.* Join three or four expanded sentences with linking words to make a paragraph.

## Section D — Speech expressions

- Greeting: *Good morning, respected principal, teachers and dear friends.*
- Introducing: *Today, I am going to speak about …*
- Opinions: *I strongly believe that …*
- Explaining: *Let me explain why …*
- Suggestions: *Let us …* · *We should …*
- Ending: *Thank you for listening.*

## Section E — Article structure

Title → (writer's name) → introduction → main ideas → supporting details → conclusion/message.

## Section F — Story tools

Characters · setting · beginning · main event · problem · resolution · ending. Starters: *One day, …* · *Last Sunday, …* · *Suddenly, …* · *After a few minutes, …* · *Finally, …* Keep the **past tense** and a clear time order.
