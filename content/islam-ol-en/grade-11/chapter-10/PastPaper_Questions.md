### A1. 2016 – Islam I, Q37

Question ID: P2016-I-Q37  
Year: 2016  
Paper and question: Islam I, Q37  
Marks: not stated on the paper (Islam I multiple-choice question)  
Source: OL_2016_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

Which is NOT one of the three places of worship that are important in Islam?

- **A.** the Ka'bah (Ka'bat Allah)
- **B.** Masjid al-Aqsa
- **C.** Masjid an-Nabawi
- **D.** Masjid al-Qiblatayn

**Correct answer:** D – Masjid al-Qiblatayn *(option (4) on the original paper)*

**Explanation:** The three sacred places of worship: Masjid al-Haram (the Ka'bah), Masjid an-Nabawi and Masjid al-Aqsa. Masjid al-Qiblatayn is not one of them.

**Question:**

(i) Introduce Masjid al-Aqsa.

**Suggested answer:**

- The third holy place of Islam, in Palestine (in the city of al-Quds).
- The second mosque, built forty years after Masjid al-Haram (Bukhari).
- The first qiblah of the Muslims; the place where the Prophet ﷺ led the prophets in prayer during the Isra' journey (17:1).
- Praying there equals five hundred prayers; one of the three mosques to which a journey for worship is permitted.
- Dawud (AS) recovered it and Sulayman (AS) enlarged it; it came into Muslim hands in 636 CE in the time of 'Umar (RA).

**Question:**

(iv) Masjid an-Nabawi

**Suggested answer:**

- The Prophet ﷺ reached Madinah on 27 September 622 CE and, while staying in the house of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (RA), began the building work on land belonging to two orphans.
- At first it was built about 35 × 30 metres with three doors; on one side was the platform of the Ashab as-Suffah.
- It was not only a place of prayer but the centre of education, da'wah, administration, giving judgement and receiving delegations.
- Praying there equals a thousand prayers; it is one of the three mosques for which a journey may be made (Bukhari).
- Later the caliphs and rulers expanded it; today it is one of the largest mosques in the world.

**Answer explanation:** Mention its history, functions and distinction.

### B5. 2023 (2024) – Islam II, Q4 (iii)

Question ID: P2023_2024-II-Q4.3  
Year: 2023 (2024)  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q4 (iii)  
Marks: 10 marks for the whole of Q4  
Source: OL_2023_2024_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(iii) Briefly explain the history of the holy Ka'bah.

**Suggested answer:**

- The first house of God set up on earth (3:96); first built by Adam (AS) (there is also the view that the angels built it).
- It was destroyed by the flood in the time of Nuh (AS).
- Ibrahim (AS) left Hajar and Isma'il in Makkah; the spring of Zamzam; later father and son rebuilt the Ka'bah together; Maqam Ibrahim (2:125, 2:127).
- 440 CE – Qusayy took Makkah and the leadership passed to the Quraysh; 'Abd al-Muttalib rediscovered the well of Zamzam; the incident of Abrahah's army of elephants.
- Rebuilt 30 years after the incident of the elephant – the Black Stone (al-Hajar al-Aswad) was placed by the Prophet's ﷺ hand.
- After the hijrah, the change of qiblah (2:144); the conquest of Makkah in 8 AH – the idols were removed and monotheism was established.

**Answer explanation:** Write in chronological order.

**Marking guidance (suggested only – not an official marking scheme):** No official marking scheme.

Year: 2024 (2025)  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q7 (iii)  
Marks: 10 marks (short notes on two topics)  
Source: OL_2024_2025_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(iii) Masjid an-Nabawi

**Suggested answer:**

- Second in spiritual rank in Islam; praying there equals 1,000 prayers (Bukhari); one of the three mosques to which a journey for worship may be made.
- Reaching Madinah in 622 CE, the Prophet ﷺ began the building work while staying in the house of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (RA).
- The place where his camel knelt, belonging to two orphans, was bought; the Prophet ﷺ worked with his Companions; walls of stone and earth, pillars of palm trunks, a roof of palm fronds; 35 m × 30 m, three doors; rooms for his wives; the Ashab as-Suffah.
- Its role: school, court, parliament, storehouse, army camp, reception hall.
- Expansion: 2,500 sq. m after the Battle of Khaybar; later by 'Umar and 'Uthman (RA), the Umayyad, Abbasid, Mamluk and Ottoman rulers, and King Fahd of Saudi Arabia – today one of the largest mosques in the world.

**Answer explanation:** Include its history, how it was built, its role and its expansion.
