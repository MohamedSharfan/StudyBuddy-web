## 2️⃣ Family background

- ⭐ **Born:** 11 June 1838, **Kandy**. He came from a family of Arab descent.
- **Mulk Rahmatullah** – came from Arabia for trade, settled in Aluthgama and married a Sri Lankan woman.
- His son **Muhammad Lebbe** settled in Kandy in the time of Sri Vikrama Rajasinghe.
  - He studied at the English school set up by the British in Kandy.
  - ⭐ **1833** – appointed a proctor (lawyer) by the British government → **Sri Lanka's first Muslim lawyer**.
  - The "Ceylon Almanac" praised him as **"the leader of the Moors of Ceylon"**.
- Siddi Lebbe was Muhammad Lebbe's **third son**.

### The family (5 children: 2 daughters, 3 sons)

| Person | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Elder brother (Muhammad Lebbe Alim) | An 'alim and a lawyer; a leader of the Muslims; a **qadi judge** who settled marriage and divorce cases; people called him "**Marakkayar**"; a poet who composed more than 300 verses |
| Sister **Muthu Nachchiya** | An 'alimah; **head teacher** of the Muslim girls' college Siddi Lebbe started on Trincomalee Street, Kandy |

## 3️⃣ Education

| From whom / where | What he learned |
|---|---|
| His elder brother Muhammad Lebbe Alim | The Quran, Tamil, Arabic, Sinhala |
| The English school, Kandy | English education |
| **Shaykh Muhammad Alim** (of Akurana – known as "Kalawatte Alim Appa Pulavar") | Further study of Arabic |

⭐ Result: mastery of three languages – **Tamil, English and Arabic**.

## 5️⃣ Marriage

- 1871 – **Sayyida Umma**, daughter of **Abdul Cader Meeya Pillai**, who came from Kayalpattinam and lived in Kandy.
- She had studied the Quran, Tamil and English; she was skilled in sewing and knitting, and could play the piano and the sitar.
- So she was able to support her husband's progressive plans.
- They had no children → he brought up a child named **Abdul Hameed**.

---

## 6️⃣ Work in journalism ⭐

| Newspaper | Year | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| **Muslim Nesan** | ⭐ **12 December 1882** (continued from 1882 to 1944) | The need for Western education, social progress, political rights, ending superstitious practices |
| **Gnanadeepam** (a monthly) | **1892** | Reviving Muslims who had grown weak in spiritual matters |

- ⭐ Through Muslim Nesan → the honour of being **"the first journalist of the Sri Lankan Muslims"**.
- Muslim Nesan was not only a paper reflecting the community's needs; it was also **a weapon** in the struggle to meet them.

---

## 7️⃣ The 'Urabi Pasha incident

- When the revolutionary **'Urabi (Arabi) Pasha**, exiled from Egypt, came to Sri Lanka, the government asked through the newspapers that he should not be welcomed.
- Siddi Lebbe ignored this, gathered the Muslims and **went to the harbour to welcome him**. He also ignored the criticism that followed.

---

## 8️⃣ Ideas that influenced him

| Spiritual side | Progressive reform side |
|---|---|
| His elder brother's guidance | 'Urabi Pasha (Egypt) |
| Kalawatte Alim Appa Pulavar | **Jamal ad-Din al-Afghani** – the unity of Muslims worldwide |
| **Abdullah Badib** of Yemen | **Sir Sayyid Ahmad Khan** – Muslim education and social development |

## 9️⃣ Educational work ⭐

- With **Wapchi Marikar** he started an **association for educational development**.
- Through speeches, newspapers and magazines he stressed that the Muslim community would progress only through education → ⭐ **Zahira College, Colombo** was founded (with the help of wealthy men such as Wapchi Marikar).
- Places where he set up schools: Colombo, Kandy, Gampola, Polgahawela, Kurunegala, Badulla, Galle, Matara.

### Education for Muslim girls

- He set up **three schools** for Muslim girls, at Trincomalee Street (Kandy), Kadai Veethi (Bazaar Street), Katukelle and Kurunegala.
  - ⚠️ The textbook names four places but says "three schools" – confirm with your teacher.
- Subjects: the Quran, Arabic + Tamil, English, mathematics, sewing.
- ⭐ **1892** – Governor **Havelock** came to visit these schools.

### His ideas on education

- Through Muslim Nesan he guided teachers and students; he drew up a syllabus and wrote textbooks.
- ⭐ Muslims should master **4 languages – Arabic, Tamil, English and Sinhala**.
- Arabic should be **the home language** of every Muslim.
- He compared someone who does not know his mother tongue, Tamil, to **a blind man**.

---

## 🔟 Literary work

- ⭐ **"Hassan Bey Charithram" (Asanbe Charithram)** – regarded as **the first Tamil novel** published in Sri Lanka; it came out as a separate book in **1885**.
- This novel, built as a story within a story, pointed out social weaknesses and aimed at revival.

---

## 1️⃣1️⃣ Work for political rights

- Awareness through Muslim Nesan and speeches.
- **1880** – the Muslims asked the Governor for Muslim representation in the Legislative Council.
- ⭐ **1889** – **M. C. Abdul Rahman** was appointed as a nominated member.

---

## 1️⃣2️⃣ Reform of marriage registration

- In Muslim Nesan he showed the serious results of marriages not being properly registered.
- ⭐ With the recommendation of the **Akbar Committee**, appointed in 1928, it came into force from **1935**.

---

## 1️⃣3️⃣ Removing superstitious practices

- European imperialists ended the healthy religious environment of the time of the Sinhala kings.
- Sri Lankan Muslims came under **the influence of South Indian culture**, and superstitions and rituals crept in.
- He used Muslim Nesan to root them out.

---

## 1️⃣4️⃣ Opposition and determination

- Wide support at national level; but **strong opposition** from conservatives and some influential people.
- He carried on his work without fear.

---

## 1️⃣5️⃣ Death and honours

- ⭐ Died in **1898**, aged 60, of **asthma**.
- More than 3,000 people attended his janazah (funeral prayer).
- Buried in the cemetery of the **Mahiyawa jungle mosque**, Kandy.
- ⭐ **11-01-1977** – the Sri Lankan government issued a **postage stamp**.
- Title: **"Father of the revival"**.

## ✅ Quick revision

- Born: 11 June 1838, Kandy; died: 1898 (asthma).
- Father Muhammad Lebbe – Sri Lanka's first Muslim lawyer (1833).
- Languages mastered: Tamil, English, Arabic.
- Muslim Nesan – 12 Dec 1882 → the first Muslim journalist.
- Gnanadeepam – 1892; Hassan Bey Charithram – the first Tamil novel (1885).
- Zahira College, Colombo – with Wapchi Marikar's help.
- 1889 – M. C. Abdul Rahman nominated to the Legislative Council.
- Marriage registration – Akbar Committee (1928) → in force 1935.
- Postage stamp – 11-01-1977.
