### A5. 2021 (2022) – Islam I, Q37

Question ID: P2021_2022-I-Q37  
Year: 2021 (2022)  
Paper and question: Islam I, Q37  
Marks: not stated on the paper (Islam I multiple-choice question)  
Source: OL_2021_2022_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

In the table below, column 'X' gives some terms connected with Islamic belief and column 'Y' their explanations.

| X | Y |
|---|---|
| (1) ikhlas | A - The universe and everything in it came into being by itself. |
| (2) ilhad | B - Muhammad ﷺ is the last prophet. |
| (3) khatam an-nabi | C - Swearing a false oath in the name of Allah. |
| (4) shirk al-asghar | D - Acting with the unmixed intention of doing it for Allah alone. |

The option that is correct when column 'Y' is arranged, in order, to match column 'X'

- **A.** A B C D
- **B.** B C D A
- **C.** D A B C
- **D.** D C B A

**Correct answer:** C – D A B C *(option (3) on the original paper)*

**Explanation:** Ikhlas – pure intention (D); ilhad – denial of God: the universe came about by itself (A); khatam an-nabi – the last prophet (B); shirk al-asghar – minor shirk (C). So D A B C.

**Answer status:** Suggested – not an official answer (no official marking scheme was supplied); based on the Grade 10/11 textbook content

**Verification note:** Matching explanation (C) with shirk al-asghar is by elimination; the textbook gives showing off, swearing by other than Allah, etc. as examples of minor shirk.

## Part B: Structured and Essay Questions (Islam II)

### B1. 2018 – Islam II, Q2 (ii)

Question ID: P2018-II-Q2.2  
Year: 2018  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q2 (ii)  
Marks: 10 marks for the whole of Q2  
Source: OL_2018_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(ii) State the two kinds of shirk akbar, with examples.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Shirk in belief (believing that others share in Allah's unique attributes such as creating and managing) – e.g. believing that some other power sends rain or cures illness.
2. Shirk in worship (performing for something other than Allah the worship due to Allah alone) – e.g. prostrating to idols or graves, making vows to them.

**Answer explanation:** Major shirk destroys good deeds (39:65); it is not forgiven without tawbah (4:48).

**Marking guidance (suggested only – not an official marking scheme):** No official marking scheme.

**Answer status:** Needs verification – a suggested answer that could not be fully confirmed from the textbook; check with a teacher

**Verification note:** The current textbook divides shirk only into "akbar and asghar"; sub-kinds of shirk akbar are not explained. The division above is based on the ideas of tawhid (creation, worship) – confirm with the teacher.

### B2. 2023 (2024) – Islam II, Q2 (i), (ii)

Question ID: P2023_2024-II-Q2.1  
Year: 2023 (2024)  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q2 (i), (ii)  
Marks: 10 marks for the whole of Q2 (division among sub-questions not given)  
Source: OL_2023_2024_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(i) Give the two main divisions of shirk, with examples.
(ii) Briefly explain three of the things that harm iman.

**Suggested answer:**

(i)
1. Shirk al-akbar (major shirk) – performing for something other than Allah the worship due to Allah alone (e.g. worshipping created things, praying to them); it is not forgiven without tawbah (4:48).
2. Shirk al-asghar (minor shirk) – e.g. swearing by other than Allah, charms, consulting fortune-tellers, showing off (riya').

(ii) Any three of the following:
1. Ilhad (atheism) – the belief that there is no God; those who say "nothing destroys us but time" (45:24).
2. Kufr (disbelief) – rejecting the foundations of Islam through belief, word or action (e.g. not believing prayer to be obligatory, insulting the Prophet, dishonouring the Quran).
3. Shirk (associating partners) – believing that others share in Allah's divinity.
4. Riddah (apostasy) – leaving Islam through a word or act that makes one a kafir.

**Answer explanation:** In (i) an example is compulsory for both divisions.

**Marking guidance (suggested only – not an official marking scheme):** No official marking scheme.

**Answer status:** Suggested – not an official answer (no official marking scheme was supplied); based on the Grade 10/11 textbook content
