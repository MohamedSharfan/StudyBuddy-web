So We destroyed them one after another and made them tales told by later generations. Allah's mercy is far from a people who do not believe.

---

## 1. What is aqeedah? ⭐
> 📖 **Aqeedah (عقيدة):** believing and accepting firmly, **without room for any doubt.**

- **In Islamic usage:** the basic Islamic beliefs called **arkan al-iman** (the pillars of faith) and matters connected with them are called aqeedah.
- **Plural:** **al-'aqa'id (العقائد).**

---

## 2. One aqeedah for all the prophets
- **All the prophets and messengers** who appeared in the world taught **the same aqeedah**.

> 📖 **(42:13 – ash-Shura) – meaning:** He has ordained for you the same religion He enjoined on Nuh – and what We have revealed to you (O Prophet), and what We enjoined on Ibrahim, Musa and 'Isa: establish the religion (din) and do not be divided in it.

### Aqeedah vs Shari'ah ⭐
| Aqeedah | Shari'ah (the law) |
|---|---|
| Has remained **the same** in every age | Has **changed** with time and place and according to circumstances |

- The people who came after the prophets **corrupted** the aqeedah → Allah sent prophets again to teach **the correct aqeedah**.
- Finally **Muhammad ﷺ** taught the aqeedah according to Allah's guidance; it is contained **in the Quran and the Sunnah**.

---

## 3. The time of the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions
- Islamic aqeedah was **in a sound state**.
- They:
  - did **not argue** about it
  - had **no doubts**
  - gave **no rational (philosophical) explanations**
  - believed and lived by the aqeedah the Messenger taught **exactly in its original form**.

---

## 4. Factors that made aqeedah lose its simplicity ⭐
In later times:
1. **Political internal conflicts** that arose within the Muslim community
2. **The ideas of outsiders**
3. Relations and contacts with **other religious groups**
4. Using **reason beyond its limits**
5. **Extremism**
6. Following **one's desires**
7. **The plots of enemies**
8. **Superstitions**

### Results
- Aqeedah lost **its simplicity**; **its life and its high standing faded.**
- Matters of aqeedah became subject to mere **philosophical disputes and argumentative debates**.
- Aqeedah, which should purify the heart, correct conduct and raise the individual and society, was in danger of becoming **a mere subject of research under philosophical inquiry**.

---

## 5. The schools of thought that appeared ⭐
Many schools of thought appeared centred on aqeedah; they put forward **deviant beliefs** contrary to pure Islamic aqeedah.

| Arabic name | In English |
|---|---|
| **الخوارج** | **The Khawarij** |
| **القدرية** | **The Qadariyyah** |
| **الجبرية** | **The Jabriyyah** |
| **المعتزلة** | **The Mu'tazilah** |
| **المرجئة** | **The Murji'ah** |
| **الشيعة** | **The Shi'ah** |

### The Shi'ah
- They appeared on the basis of a **political dispute** over **who was entitled to the caliphate** after the Prophet ﷺ.
- Later they grew into **a group with a differing aqeedah**.

---

## 6. The imams who protected the purity of aqeedah ⭐
1. **Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal** (RH)
2. **Imam Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari** (RH)
3. **Abu Mansur al-Maturidi** (RH)
4. **Abu Ja'far at-Tahawi** (RH)

### The sacrifice of Imam Ahmad (RH)
- He struggled against the ideas of **the Mu'tazilah**.
- He said (meaning):
  > "**It is better that Islam lives and I die, than that I live and Islam dies.**"
- This is **evidence of the sacrifice** he made to protect the purity of aqeedah.

👉 **History confirms** that whenever ideas against pure aqeedah appear, people arise who protect and guide the ummah.

---

## ✅ Quick revision
- Aqeedah = firm belief without doubt; plural – al-'aqa'id; arkan al-iman.
- One aqeedah for all the prophets (42:13); the Shari'ah changed with the times.
- The time of the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions: no disputes, no doubts, no rational explanations.
- Factors: political conflict, outside ideas, contact with religious groups, excessive use of reason, extremism, desires, enemy plots, superstitions.
- Groups: Khawarij, Qadariyyah, Jabriyyah, Mu'tazilah, Murji'ah, Shi'ah.
- Imams: Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari, Abu Mansur al-Maturidi, Abu Ja'far at-Tahawi.
