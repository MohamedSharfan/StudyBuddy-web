## 1. The relationship between the Arabs and Sri Lanka ⭐

### Reasons for the relationship
- Sri Lanka's **location**, and the Arabs' **knowledge and experience of sea travel** → contact **even before the Christian era began**.
- Even before the rise of Islam, Arab traders traded in **the Indian Ocean region**; they were **agents** in the exchange of goods between East and West.
- Sri Lanka lay **in the middle** of the trade route linking East and West → **a stopping point** for Arab trade voyages.
- Facilities that made Arab traders stop there: **harbours, trade goods, security, food, drinking water, ship-repair facilities.**
- They were keen to set up **settlements** in South Asia and South-East Asia.

### Historical evidence ⭐
| Evidence | Details |
|---|---|
| From before **310 BCE** | In trade between **Madagascar and Sumatra**, they used Sri Lanka as **a middle zone** |
| **Pliny (23–79 CE)** | The Arabs on **the Malabar coast and in Sri Lanka** were **experts** in maritime activities; large numbers in Malabar, **a small number** in Sri Lanka |
| **Ptolemy (150 CE)** | The knowledge of Sri Lanka in his **map** was obtained **from the seafaring Arabs** |

👉 The Arab–Sri Lanka relationship existed **from before the rise of Islam**; after the coming of Islam it became **even stronger.**

## 2. When Islam was introduced to Sri Lanka ⭐
- Although there is no clear dated evidence, it can be inferred from **reliable evidence**.
- In his last years the Prophet ﷺ sent the message of Islam to **many countries of the world** (his letters are evidence); historians say letters were sent **to the eastern countries** too.

### (1) The Prophet's ﷺ time – Wahb ibn Abi Habsa ⭐
| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Thomas Arnold** | A Companion named **Wahb ibn Habsa** went to **China** carrying the Prophet's message |
| **M. C. Siddi Lebbe – "Muslim Nesan"** | "In **628 CE** the Prophet ﷺ sent a Companion named **Wahb ibn Abi Habsa** **to the king of Sri Lanka**; the Companion who delivered the letter was given **permission to carry out his work**." He returned home in **632 CE** |

👉 These two pieces of evidence support the view that Islam was introduced to Sri Lanka **in the Prophet's ﷺ own time**.

> ⚠️ **Note:** English-language works (e.g. Thomas Arnold's *The Preaching of Islam*) give this Companion's name as **Wahb ibn Abi Kabshah**. The spelling "Habsa" follows the Tamil textbook. Use the textbook form in the exam.

### (2) The time of Caliph 'Umar (RA) – Ibn Shahriyar ⭐
- The historian **Ibn Shahriyar – the book "'Aja'ib al-Hind"**:
  - The Arabs settled in Sri Lanka sent **a capable man, with another as his companion**, to learn correct information about the Prophet ﷺ.
  - When the envoy reached Arabia, the Prophet ﷺ and **the first caliph had died, and the second caliph** was ruling.
  - He learned about Islam from **'Umar (RA)** and set off back; on the way he died **on the Makran coast (Pakistan)**; the **servant** who went with him reached Sri Lanka and passed on the information about Islam.

### (3) 8th century CE – the Banu Hashim settlement
- In the reign of **the Umayyad caliphs 'Abd al-Malik and al-Walid**, fearing the cruelty of **al-Hajjaj, governor of Iraq**, the **Banu Hashim** – regarded as enemies of the Umayyad state – settled on **the coasts of South India and Sri Lanka**.
- Evidence: **Sir James Emerson Tennent – the book "Ceylon"**.

### (4) Early 8th century CE – the conquest of Sindh ⭐
- Evidence: the historian **al-Baladhuri – the book "Futuh al-Buldan"**.
- The Sri Lankan king **Dathopatissa II** sent **the widows and orphaned children** of Arab traders who had died in Sri Lanka back to Arabia.
- They and the ship were captured **by pirates** at **Debal, near present-day Karachi**.
- **Al-Hajjaj** wrote to **Raja Dahir**, king of Sindh, asking him to free the women; the request was **ignored**.
- An army was sent under **Muhammad ibn Qasim**, and **Sindh** was conquered by the Muslims in **715 CE**.

### (5) Other evidence
- **Arab coins** found in Sri Lanka confirm that Muslims lived in Sri Lanka in **the 2nd and 3rd centuries AH**.
- **Gravestones and inscriptions:**
  - The gravestone of **Khalid ibn Bakaya**, set up in **337 AH**, was found in **1787**.
  - At the request of the Sri Lankan Muslims he was sent **to Colombo by the caliph in Baghdad (Abbasid)** to teach Islam, and did religious work there; the stone was found **during the British period**.

## 3. The good character and social position of the early Muslims ⭐
- The kings and people of Sri Lanka **welcomed them and treated them hospitably.**
- To make import and export easier, their settlements and **warehouses** were **on the coast**.
- They brought in needed **foreign goods**, and took **local products to the international market**, **introducing** both the goods and the country at international level.
- They behaved **courteously** with the local kings and people; **medical and transport services.**
- **They had no intention at all of occupying the land or plundering its resources.**

### Results
- **A monopoly** in Sri Lanka's **import and export trade**; they became **advisers to the government** on trade.
- For their services they were given **separate residential areas**; they won the hearts of the local people so much that **local women were given to them in marriage**.
- High posts such as **minister and ambassador under the Sinhala kings**.
- The Islamic **caliphate** too **kept watch** over the Sri Lankan Muslims (the example of Khalid ibn Bakaya).

### Areas of settlement
| At first (coastal towns) | Later (gradually) |
|---|---|
| **Colombo, Beruwala, Galle, Matara** | **Kurunegala, Gampola, the central hill country** |

👉 Their descendants are the Muslim community living today in many parts of the country.

---

## 4. Taking part in the national good ⭐

### The economy
- They carried Sri Lanka's **gems, ivory and spices** abroad.
- Arab traders and pilgrims, who believed that **the mountain bearing the footprint of Adam (AS) is in Sri Lanka**, made the island **famous** through their travels.
- A great contribution to earning **foreign exchange** through **exports** → as people began to produce, **the country's income increased.**
- They also worked **for agricultural improvement**, and took part in farming themselves.
- They brought **weavers** from abroad and developed weaving.
- They introduced and managed the transport system called **"tavalam"** (caravans of pack animals).

### Politics and diplomacy
| Event | Details |
|---|---|
| **Parakramabahu the Great (1156–1186)** – Polonnaruwa | **4 Muslims were appointed as trade advisers** at his court – Professor **S. Paranavitana** confirms what the geographer **al-Idrisi** reports |
| **Ambassador to Egypt** | The Sri Lankan government sent a Muslim who took part in the import–export trade **as ambassador to Egypt** |
| **King Mayadunne** | To **drive out the Portuguese**, he sent Muslims **as envoys** to obtain the help of **the Zamorin**, king of Calicut; the Muslims **obtained military help and also fought themselves** |
| **The Kandyan king** | Muslims also served as his envoys |
| **The British period** | They worked in the country's **administration** too |

### The struggle for independence ⭐
- In **the later part of the 19th century** they struggled for the country's independence together with the other communities.
- When the British, with their **divide-and-rule policy**, tried to divide the communities and delay independence, Muslim leaders including **T. B. Jayah** joined the majority community in speaking out, which made gaining independence **easier**.
- The **fair arguments** of Muslim politicians were among the factors through which self-rule, ended in **1815**, was regained in **1948**.

### Other fields
**Social harmony, educational development, the judiciary, health, international relations** – the contribution of Muslims, then and now, is unforgettable.
