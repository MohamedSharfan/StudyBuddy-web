- What fiqh is

> 📖 **Opening hadith of the chapter (Bukhari, Muslim):** مَنْ يُرِدِ اللهُ بِهِ خَيْرًا يُفَقِّهْهُ فِي الدِّيْنِ
> **Meaning:** "When Allah wants good for someone, He gives him **understanding of the religion**."

---

## 1. What is fiqh? ⭐
- Among the many sciences that appeared in Islamic history, **fiqh is the foremost**; it is **the first** of the Islamic sciences **to appear.**
- **"Fiqh" (الفقه)** = **understanding.**
- The laws of the Shari'ah derived on the basis of **the Quran, the Sunnah and ijtihad** are contained in fiqh.

---

## 2. The origin and growth of the science of fiqh ⭐

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| **The time of the Tabi'un** | Fiqh began to grow as **a separate science**; famous mujtahids held **classes explaining fiqh** in the main cities of the Islamic state; rulings were given on new problems through ijtihad |
| **The time of the Tabi' al-Tabi'in** | **The golden age** of fiqh; rulings on many new problems → these were collected into **books of fiqh law** → **the madhhabs** appeared |

- Although many madhhabs appeared in history, for various reasons **four madhhabs remained**; they are named after **the imams** who founded them.
- Their efforts made it **easy for ordinary people, the muqallids**, to live according to Islam.

> 📌 **Muqallid (مقلّد)** = an ordinary person who follows the rulings of the mujtahids. **Mujtahid** = a scholar qualified to make ijtihad.

---

## 3. The four imams ⭐

### (1) Imam Abu Hanifah (RH) – 80–150 AH
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Own name** | **Nu'man ibn Thabit** |
| **Birthplace** | **Kufah** |
| **Title** | **Al-Imam al-A'zam (the Greatest Imam)** |
| Occupation | **A trader**; yet keen to study the religious sciences |
| **Teacher** | **Hammad ibn Abi Sulayman**, the famous scholar of Kufah; he also learned from the scholars of Makkah and Madinah on his hajj journeys; after Hammad's death he **took over** the class |
| **Book** | His rulings were recorded and published under the name **"al-Fiqh al-Akbar"*** |
| **Way of thinking** | In his time **the reliability of hadiths was unclear**, so he judged by ijtihad, using as evidence the hadiths he found to be sound → **"madrasat ar-ra'y" (the school of reasoned opinion)** |
| **Students** | **Imam Abu Yusuf, Zufar, al-Hasan ash-Shaybani** – they refined their teacher's views by linking them with hadiths |
| **Madhhab** | **The Hanafi madhhab**; because Imam Abu Yusuf served as **chief judge** in the time of **Caliph Harun**, it was declared **the state madhhab** in Caliph Harun's time |

> ⚠️ *"Al-Fiqh al-Akbar" is generally known as a short work on aqeedah attributed to Abu Hanifah; the student usually named alongside Abu Yusuf and Zufar is Muhammad ibn al-Hasan ash-Shaybani – confirm with the teacher.

### (2) Imam Malik (RH) – 93–179 AH
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Own name** | **Malik ibn Anas** |
| **Birthplace** | **Madinah** |
| **Title** | **Imam al-Madinah (the Imam of Madinah)** |
| Time | **A contemporary** of Imam Abu Hanifah; he learned hadith and fiqh from **the Tabi'i scholars** of Madinah |
| Teaching | In Madinah; students, the public and sometimes **even caliphs** attended |
| **Way of thinking** | Living in Madinah, **hadiths were easy to obtain** → solutions based on the Quran and the Sunnah → **"madrasat al-athar" (the school of hadith-based tradition)** |
| **Book** | **Al-Muwatta'** |
| **Madhhab** | **The Maliki madhhab**; introduced into **Spain** by his student **Yahya al-Andalusi**; it became **the state madhhab** there in the time of **Caliph al-Hakam II** |
| Influence | Notable influence **in North and West African countries** |

### (3) Imam ash-Shafi'i (RH) – 150–204 AH
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Own name** | **Muhammad ibn Idris** |
| **Birthplace** | **Gaza, Palestine** |
| Lineage | **Quraysh** |
| **Title** | **'Alim Quraysh (the scholar of the Quraysh)** |
| Education | **Makkah and Madinah**; taught **in Baghdad**; served the state **in Yemen**; worked as **mufti and teacher** in Baghdad; when **the interference of the Mu'tazilah** increased he went to **Egypt** |
| Talents | Besides the Sunnah and fiqh, **Arabic literature and medicine**; also famous **as a poet** |
| **Books** | **Musnad al-Imam ash-Shafi'i, Kitab al-Umm, ar-Risalah (on usul)** |
| **Teachers** | **Imam Malik**, and **Imam Abu Yusuf*** of the Hanafi madhhab |
| **Way of thinking** | He took in **both madrasat al-athar and madrasat ar-ra'y**, so his approach was **progressive (balanced)** |
| **Rulings** | Those given **in Baghdad** = **qadim (old)**; those given **in Egypt** = **jadid (new)** |
| **Madhhab** | **The Shafi'i madhhab**; **the state madhhab in Egypt** in the time of **Sultan Salah ad-Din** |
| Followers | Most Muslims in countries such as **Sri Lanka, Indonesia, Malaysia and Egypt** |

> ⚠️ *Biographies usually name Muhammad ibn al-Hasan ash-Shaybani as the Hanafi scholar ash-Shafi'i studied with – confirm with the teacher.

### (4) Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal (RH) – 164–241 AH
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Own name** | **Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal** |
| **Birthplace** | **Baghdad** (he grew up there) |
| **Teachers** | **Imam ash-Shafi'i, Imam Abu Yusuf** |
| Fields | Contributed in both **hadith and fiqh** |
| **Book** | **Musnad Ahmad** |
| Background | At a time when the views of **the Mu'tazilah**, who arose under **the influence of Greek thought**, had spread, he put forward ideas based on the Quran and the Sunnah |
| **Way of thinking** | **"Madrasat al-athar"** |
| **Madhhab** | **The Hanbali madhhab**; **the state madhhab** in the time of the Abbasid **Caliph al-Mutawakkil** |
| Influence | **Officially recognised in Saudi Arabia**; influential in the Arabian Peninsula |

- Besides these four, **many other scholars** contributed to fiqh; their views are also considered when seeking solutions to legal problems.

## 5. Madrasat al-athar vs madrasat ar-ra'y ⭐

| | **Madrasat al-athar (مدرسة الأثر)** | **Madrasat ar-ra'y (مدرسة الرأي)** |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Solutions based on **the Quran and the Sunnah (hadith)** | Solutions through **ijtihad / reasoned opinion**, together with the hadiths found to be sound |
| Imams | **Malik, Ahmad** | **Abu Hanifah** |
| Combined by | **Ash-Shafi'i** – took in both |

### Mujtahid vs muqallid
| Mujtahid | Muqallid |
|---|---|
| One who derives rulings from the Quran and Sunnah through ijtihad | An ordinary person who follows the rulings of the mujtahids |

## 6. Key Arabic terms
| Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| الفقه | Fiqh – understanding; the science of Islamic law |
| التفسير | Tafsir – commentary on the Quran |
| المعروفات / المنكرات | Good things / evil things |
| مسألة فقهية | A question of fiqh |
| مذهب | Madhhab – a school of legal thought |
| الإجماع / القياس | Ijma' / qiyas |
| مدرسة الأثر / مدرسة الرأي | The school of athar (hadith) / the school of ra'y (reasoned opinion) |

## ✅ Quick revision
- Fiqh = understanding; the first Islamic science to appear.
- Time of the Tabi'un – grew as a separate science; time of the Tabi' al-Tabi'in – the golden age, the madhhabs appeared.
- Abu Hanifah: Nu'man ibn Thabit, Kufah, al-Imam al-A'zam, Hammad, al-Fiqh al-Akbar, ra'y, Harun.
- Malik: Malik ibn Anas, Madinah, Imam al-Madinah, al-Muwatta', athar, Yahya al-Andalusi – Spain – al-Hakam II.
- Ash-Shafi'i: Muhammad ibn Idris, Gaza, 'Alim Quraysh, al-Umm, ar-Risalah, qadim/jadid, Salah ad-Din, the majority in Sri Lanka.
- Ibn Hanbal: Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal, Baghdad, al-Musnad, athar, al-Mutawakkil, Saudi Arabia.
