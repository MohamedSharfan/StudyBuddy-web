### A1. 2016 – Islam I, Q25

Question ID: P2016-I-Q25  
Year: 2016  
Paper and question: Islam I, Q25

Source: OL_2016_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

Ahmad was carrying water in a vessel to his father. Suddenly he bumped into his sister Naseema, who came there with a cup of tea, and the tea she was carrying spilled into Ahmad's water vessel. What is this water called?

- **A.** It becomes 'al-ma' at-tahir'.
- **B.** It becomes 'al-ma' at-tahur'.
- **C.** It becomes 'al-ma' al-musta'mal'.
- **D.** It becomes 'al-ma' an-najis'.

**Correct answer:** A – It becomes 'al-ma' at-tahir'. *(option (1) on the original paper)*

**Explanation:** Because a clean substance (tea) mixed with it and changed the nature of the water, it is clean in itself but does not purify other things – al-ma' at-tahir.

### B1. 2016 – Islam II, Q7 (i)

Question ID: P2016-II-Q7.1  
Year: 2016  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q7 (i)  
Marks: 10 marks (short notes on two topics)  
Source: OL_2016_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

Write short notes on two of the following.
(i) The kinds of water and their uses

**Suggested answer:**

Jurists divide water into three:
1. Al-ma' at-tahur (purifying water) – clean in itself and purifies other things. E.g. rain, sea, river and spring water. Use: wudu, obligatory ghusl, washing the dead, removing najis. It is divided into a large amount (mutlaq), a small amount (qalil) and used water (musta'mal).
2. Al-ma' at-tahir (clean water) – clean in itself but does not purify other things. E.g. tea, fruit juice, king coconut water. Use: food, medicine; it cannot be used for wudu or ghusl.
3. Al-ma' al-mutanajjis (impure water) – impure in itself and does not purify other things. E.g. drain water. It cannot be used for religious duties.

**Answer explanation:** Each kind needs a definition, an example and its use.
