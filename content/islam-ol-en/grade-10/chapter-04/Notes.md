---

## 1. The importance of cleanliness
- Islam is a guide for the whole of life. It insists that every Muslim keeps clean.
- Keeping clean is itself an **act of worship (ibadah) that brings reward**.

| Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Quran (9:108)** | Allah certainly **loves those who keep themselves pure.** |
| **Quran (74:4)** – from **Surah al-Muddaththir**, revealed early | (O Prophet!) **Keep your clothes clean.** |
| **Hadith (Muslim)** | **Cleanliness is half of Iman.** |
| **Hadith (Muslim)** | Allah does not accept any prayer **without purification**. |

- Cleanliness is a **basic condition** for the main acts of worship, such as prayer and *i'tikaf* (staying in the mosque for worship).

### Outward and inward cleanliness
- **Outward cleanliness:** keeping the body, clothes and place free from impurities.
- **Inward cleanliness:** keeping the heart free from bad qualities such as **jealousy, pride, suspicion and hatred**.

👉 Islam teaches us to care about both equally.

## 2. Types of water ⭐
**Water** is essential for outward cleanliness. Jurists divide water into three types:

| Type | Arabic name | Clean in itself? | Can it purify other things? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Purifying water | **Al-ma' al-tahur** (الماء الطهور) | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes |
| 2. Clean water mixed with clean things | **Al-ma' al-tahir** (الماء الطاهر) | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| 3. Impure water | **Al-ma' al-mutanajjis** (الماء المتنجس) | ❌ No | ❌ No |

---

### 2.1 Al-ma' al-tahur (purifying water)
- It is clean in itself and can also purify other things.
- There is **no change in its natural state**.

**Examples:**
- Water from precipitation (rain, snow, hail)
- Sea water
- River water and spring water
- A large amount of water that has changed because leaves, moss and so on have collected in it over a long time (e.g. water in a tank/pond)

**Uses:** wudu, the obligatory ghusl, washing the dead body (*janazah*), removing najis.

#### Three kinds of tahur water

| Kind | Explanation | If najis falls into it | Using it again |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(1) Al-ma' al-kathir** (a large amount of water) – also called **al-ma' al-mutlaq** | Water more than the amount of **qullatayn** (according to the textbook, qullatayn ≈ **210 litres**) | It becomes najis **only if one** of its colour, smell or taste changes | If the change disappears by itself or by adding more water, it becomes tahur again |
| **(2) Al-ma' al-qalil** (a small amount of water) | Water less than qullatayn | It **becomes najis even if there is no change** (it does not become najis if a fly, ant or bee – creatures without flowing blood – falls into it) | It may be used after adding water up to qullatayn, provided there is no change in colour, smell or taste |
| **(3) Al-ma' al-musta'mal** (used water) | Water less than qullatayn that has been used for an **obligatory ghusl or wudu** | — | It cannot be used for another obligatory act. If clean water is added to make it qullatayn, it may be used again |

> ⚠️ Verification: the wording about qullatayn in the textbook is unclear (listed in `VERIFY.md`, Grade 10, Chapter 4). The figure of about 210 litres is as given in the textbook.

💡 **Easy way to remember:**
- **A large amount of water** → "najis only if it changes"
- **A small amount of water** → "najis as soon as najis touches it"
- **Used water** → "not for another obligatory act"

---

### 2.2 Al-ma' al-tahir (clean water)
- It is clean in itself, but **cannot purify other things.**
- Its colour, smell or taste has changed because clean things have been mixed into it.
- **Examples:** tea, rose water, fruit juice, soft drinks, **king coconut water** (changed by nature).
- It may be used for food, medicine and similar needs, **but not for religious duties such as wudu and ghusl.**

### 2.3 Al-ma' al-mutanajjis (impure water)
- It is not clean in itself and cannot purify other things.
- **Examples:** liquids that are najis by nature, such as urine; water mixed with najis, such as **sewage water**.
- It cannot be used for religious duties or for cleaning.

## 3. Hadath (ritual impurity) ⭐
> 📖 **Hadath (حدث):** the state a Muslim enters when he has **minor or major ritual impurity**.

| Impurity | How one becomes pure | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Minor hadath** | **Wudu** | Washing or wiping (*mash*) parts of the body such as the face, hands, head and feet |
| **Major hadath** | **Ghusl** (ritual bath) | Washing the whole body with water |

### When ghusl becomes obligatory
1. Sexual intercourse between a man and a woman
2. Discharge of semen
3. The end of *hayd* (menstruation)
4. The end of *nifas* (bleeding after childbirth)
5. Entering Islam
6. Death

⭐ Taking a ghusl and performing wudu to remove hadath are themselves **acts of worship**, and they bring reward.

---

## 4. Concessions in purification
Islam is an **easy and practical** religion. If something prevents wudu or ghusl, there are concessions. The main ones are **tayammum and jabirah** (both are confirmed by the Quran and the Sunnah).

### 4.1 Tayammum (تيمم)
> 📖 When water cannot be used for the obligatory wudu or ghusl, **wiping (mash) the face and both hands using clean soil**.

**When tayammum is done:**
- When water cannot be used because of illness
- When there is no water
- In situations where water cannot be used
- When there is only enough water for drinking

👉 After tayammum, one may perform prayer and other acts of worship.

### 4.2 Jabirah (جبيرة)
> 📖 A **bandage or plaster** put on a part of the body because of a wound.

- If such a bandage is on one of the parts washed in wudu, **tayammum may be done instead of washing** that place.
- **The other parts of wudu must be washed with water**.

## ✅ Quick revision
- Cleanliness is half of Iman (Muslim); Allah loves those who keep themselves pure (9:108); keep your clothes clean (74:4).
- Three types of water: **tahur** (clean + purifies), **tahir** (clean only), **mutanajjis** (impure).
- Three kinds of tahur water: **kathir (mutlaq), qalil, musta'mal.**
- Hadath: minor hadath → wudu; major hadath → ghusl.
- Concessions: **tayammum** (face and hands with soil), **jabirah** (a bandage over a wound).
