# Grade 10 – Chapter 21: The Islamic View of Economics – Quiz

> Source: `StudyBuddy_Islam/Grade_10/Chapter_21/Quiz.md` (Tamil), Q1–Q16 in the same order. Answer letters unchanged.

---

Q1. What does "economics" refer to?

A. A method of saving wealth
B. Trade only
C. Banking
D. The science that studies the economic effort made to obtain human needs

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: The effort made to obtain human needs is "economic effort"; the science that studies this effort is "economics".

Difficulty: Easy

---

Q2. Which of the following is NOT a basic human need?

A. Recreation
B. Clothing
C. Shelter
D. Food

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Food, clothing and shelter are basic needs. Education, security, telecommunications and recreation are needs that arose as society developed.

Difficulty: Easy

---

Q3. Which idea rejects individuals' economic interest and makes all property public property?

A. Capitalism
B. Socialism
C. The Islamic economy
D. Feudalism

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Socialism makes property public. Capitalism gives most importance to individual interest. Both are unbalanced.

Difficulty: Easy

---

Q4. What is the main reason the Islamic economy differs from capitalism and socialism?

A. It looks only at profit
B. It brings harmony between the interest of society and the interest of the individual
C. It forbids private property
D. It forbids trade

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Islam presents a balanced economic system that balances individual interest and the interest of society.

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q5. Into how many kinds are the foundations of the Islamic economy divided?

A. Five
B. Three – production, distribution, consumption
C. Four – land, labour, capital, enterprise
D. Two – doctrinal and practical

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: The foundations of the Islamic economy: 1) doctrinal (based on belief) 2) practical.

Difficulty: Easy

---

Q6. Which verse is the evidence for the principle "society also has a share in an individual's property"?

A. 64:15
B. 102:8
C. 70:24–25
D. 2:29

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: 70:24–25 – "In their wealth there is a known share for the one who asks and the one who is deprived."

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q7. "Your wealth and your children are only a test" (64:15) – which principle does this explain?

A. Writing things down
B. Resources belong to Allah
C. The prohibition of interest
D. Wealth is given to test a person

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Wealth is a test; in the Hereafter we will be questioned about how we earned and spent it (102:8).

Difficulty: Easy

---

Q8. Which verse says we must keep moderation in spending and saving?

A. 17:29
B. 2:275
C. 2:282
D. 9:60

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: 17:29 – do not tie your hand to your neck (miserliness), nor stretch it out completely (waste).

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q9. To what does the Quran (2:275) compare those who consume interest?

A. A mirage
B. The braying of a donkey
C. The rising of one driven mad by Satan's touch
D. A spider's web

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: 2:275 says that those who consume interest will not stand except as one stands who has been driven mad by Satan's touch.

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q10. Which verse forbids going to the authorities with bribes to seize other people's property?

A. 2:279
B. 2:188
C. 59:7
D. 27:40

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Verse 2:188 forbids consuming another's property wrongfully and seizing it through bribery.

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q11. Which verse says that when you give a loan for a fixed term you should write it down?

A. 2:275
B. 2:188
C. 2:282
D. 9:60

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: 2:282 is the evidence that contractual economic dealings must be witnessed and written down.

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q12. Which foundation makes the Islamic economic system suitable for every time, place and situation?

A. Forbidding interest
B. Islamic scholars solving new problems through ijtihad
C. Paying zakat
D. Writing things down

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Because new needs and problems that arise with changing times can be solved through ijtihad, the Islamic economy fits every age.

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q13. "So that wealth does not circulate only among the rich among you…" (59:7) – which feature of the Islamic economy does this show?

A. Divine nature
B. Flexibility
C. Correcting economic inequalities in society
D. Spiritual needs

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Islam's excellent system of distribution prevents wealth from piling up with a few, and corrects inequalities.

Difficulty: Medium

---

Q14. "You will not wrong, nor be wronged" (2:279) – this is the evidence for which feature?

A. No injustice to anyone
B. Flexibility
C. Divine nature
D. Being grateful

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: A main aim of Islamic economic activity is that no one should suffer injustice.

Difficulty: Easy

---

Q15. What is the reason for the "flexibility" of the Islamic economy?

A. Economic activities belong to mu'amalat; apart from the few things Islam forbids, everything else is permitted
B. Everything is forbidden
C. It was made by human beings
D. It looks only at profit

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: In mu'amalat (dealings) the basic rule is permission. New matters can be resolved through ijtihad within the Shari'ah.

Difficulty: Hard

---

Q16. Regarding "spiritual and worldly needs", what does the textbook say is the difference between other economic theories and the Islamic economy?

A. The others give importance only to profit; the Islamic economy gives importance to values, morals, culture and civilisation along with profit
B. Islam forbids profit
C. The two are the same
D. Islam looks only at the spiritual

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Islam does not forbid profit; but it gives moral values equal importance alongside profit.

Difficulty: Medium
