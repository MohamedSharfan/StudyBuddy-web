# Grade 10 – Chapter 5: An-Najasah (Impurity)

> Source: `StudyBuddy_Islam/Grade_10/Chapter_05/Notes.md` (Tamil). English rendering; status: sample batch, awaiting review.

## 🎯 What you will learn
- What *najasah* means
- The three types of *najis* (impure substances)
- How each type is cleaned
- Cases where impure things can be cleaned and used

---

## 1. Introduction
- **An-Najasah (النجاسة)** is an Arabic word meaning **impurity**. An impure substance is called **najis**.
- To stay clean and healthy, a Muslim must always **keep away from najis**.
- Scholars divide najis into **three types** according to **how it is cleaned**.

| Type | Examples | How it is cleaned (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Ordinary najis** | Faeces, urine, blood… | Remove the najis, then wash until its taste, colour and smell are gone |
| 2. **Light najis** | Urine of a **baby boy** under 2 years old who is fed **only on breast milk** | Squeeze it out, then **sprinkle** water over the place |
| 3. **Severe najis** | Anything from dogs and pigs | Wash **seven times**, once with **water mixed with soil** |

---

## 2. Ordinary najis

### What it includes
- Faeces, urine, blood, pus, vomit
- *Madhy* (pre-seminal fluid) and *wady* (thick white fluid discharged after urination)
- Alcoholic drinks
- Saliva that flows from the mouth during deep sleep
- Milk of animals that are not permitted to be eaten
- Dead bodies of animals
- Parts cut off from living animals

### ⚠️ Exceptions (these are NOT najis)
- **Fish and locusts that die by themselves** (they are permitted to be eaten)
- Creatures that **have no flowing blood**, such as ants and bees
- **Bones, horns, nails, hair and feathers** of dead animals that are permitted to be eaten
- **A very small amount** of blood on clothes or the body
- **Breast milk**

### How to clean it
1. First, **remove** the najis from the place.
2. **Wash** until all three of its features – **taste, colour and smell** – are gone.
3. If water alone cannot remove it, **soap or washing powder** may be used.

> ⚠️ If, after cleaning, **both colour and smell remain, or taste alone remains**, the place is **not** considered clean.

---

## 3. Light najis
- The urine of a **baby boy under two years old** who takes **only breast milk** as food.

### How to clean it
- After **squeezing out** the najis, it is enough to **sprinkle a sufficient amount** of clean water over the place.

💡 Remember all three conditions: **baby boy + under 2 years + only breast milk**. If even one condition is missing, it is not light najis.

---

## 4. Severe najis
- Everything from **dogs and pigs**, such as their faeces, urine, saliva, sweat and blood.

### How to clean it
1. Follow the method used for ordinary najis, and
2. Use **water mixed with soil** (in the first washing or in one of the middle washings), and
3. Pour water and wash **seven times**.

> ⚠️ Washing seven times is **compulsory even if** none of the three features (colour, smell, taste) of the najis **can be seen**.

### Cleaning in a large body of water or running water
- When cleaning in a **large body of water** (such as the sea or a tank/pond) **or in running water**: stir the water a little, dip the item in it, and then rinse it **six times** in the water. This is sufficient.

> ⚠️ Verification: the "six times" figure is given as stated in the Tamil textbook (see `VERIFY.md`, Grade 10, Chapter 5). Confirm with a teacher.

---

## 5. Cleaning and using impure things
In general, things that are najis in themselves cannot be made clean. However, there are some exceptions:

| Item | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Animal skin** | The skin of any animal **except dogs and pigs** may be used for purposes other than food once it has been properly **tanned** |
| **Alcoholic drink (toddy)** | If toddy with nothing else added turns **into vinegar by itself**, it may be used as food |
| **Solid dairy food with najis on it** | If najis falls on **solid** ghee, butter or cheese, remove the part touched by the najis and use the rest |

---

## ✅ Quick revision
- Najasah = impurity; 3 types: **ordinary, light, severe**.
- Ordinary → remove it, then wash until taste, colour and smell are gone.
- Light → urine of a baby boy under 2 who drinks only breast milk → sprinkling water is enough.
- Severe → dogs and pigs → wash 7 times, once with water mixed with soil; in a large body of water or running water, rinse 6 times.
- Exceptions: fish and locusts that die by themselves; creatures without flowing blood; breast milk; a very small amount of blood.
- Can be cleaned and used: tanned skin (except dogs and pigs), toddy that turns into vinegar by itself, solid dairy foods (remove the affected part).
