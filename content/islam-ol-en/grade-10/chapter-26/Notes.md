## 1. Introduction
- Islam has laid down principles and laws for a person's **individual, family and social** life.
- For a person to live as a human being, **duties must be fulfilled and rights must be received**.
- As Allah's most honoured creation, **all human beings are equal** – whatever their time, surroundings, country, race, or political, social, economic or educational position.
- **Allah alone** is able to give them **common, balanced and unbiased** duties and rights.
- So Islam takes human rights **from the Quran and the Sunnah**.

### Rights given by Allah = birthrights
- No one can **deny, take away or misuse** them.
- Those who do so are **great traitors** and **deserve Allah's punishment.**

---

## 2. Why are rights made by human beings weak? ⭐

| Rights made by human beings ❌ | Rights given by Islam ✅ |
|---|---|
| They **differ with time and place** | They are **permanent** |
| They are **taken away** just as they are given | **No one can take them away** |
| They exist **on paper only**; **they do not work in practice** | They **work continuously** |
| They are **denied** by those in power; they **expire** | They **suit human nature** |
| **Human equality suffers** | Denying them is not only illegal but **a sin and punishable** |

## 3. Historical charters ⭐

| Charter | Time | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **The Charter of Madinah** | **At the start** of the Prophet's ﷺ **hijrah to Madinah** | An agreement made with the people living there – Islam proclaimed human rights **six centuries before the West thought of them** |
| **The Magna Carta (the Great Charter)** | **1215** – **six centuries later** | An agreement **in England between King John** and the people; **many changes** were made to it over time |

> ⚠️ The Tamil textbook calls the king "John II" (irandam Jon). The king who agreed to the Magna Carta in 1215 is usually known simply as King John of England – confirm with the teacher.

### Dr C. G. Weeramantry
- **A former judge (Justice).** Book: **"Islamic Jurisprudence: An International Perspective."**
- His view: if, without considering any of today's international charters, one studied **only Islamic legal literature**, collected the human rights in it and drew up an **"International Islamic Declaration of Human Rights"**, it would be **comparable to today's human rights charter**.

> ⚠️ The Tamil textbook gives his initials as "C. V."; the author of this book is generally known as C. G. Weeramantry – confirm with the teacher.

## 4. Human rights given by Islam (7) ⭐

### (1) The right to life
- Human life is **sacred** and must be protected.
- **(6:151) – meaning:** Do not kill a soul that Allah has made sacred, **except by due process of law**.
- **(5:32) – meaning:** Whoever kills a soul unjustly, it is as if he **has killed all mankind**; and whoever saves a life, it is as if he **has saved all mankind**.

### (2) The right to equality
- All people are **equal before the law**; they cannot be treated differently because of **race, place, colour or appearance**.
- **Hadith (Bayhaqi) – meaning:** Your Lord is One; **an Arab is not better than a non-Arab, nor a white person better than a black person**; you are ranked higher **only through God-consciousness (taqwa)**.

### (3) The right to individual freedom
- **Hadith (Bukhari) – meaning:** I will be the opponent of three kinds of people on the Day of Judgement; one of them is **a person who enslaves a free person, sells him and eats from the price.**
- So Islam **encourages freeing slaves and forbids the slave trade.**
- **Freedom of religion – (2:256) – meaning:** "There is **no compulsion** in religion; right guidance has become clear from error."

### (4) The right to justice
- Everyone has the right **to stand equally before the law and to receive equal justice**.
- **(5:8) – meaning:** Stand firm for Allah as just witnesses; do not let **hatred of a people** lead you to be unjust to them; justice is **closest to God-consciousness**.
- The Prophet ﷺ (meaning): "**Even if my daughter Fatimah stole**, I would not hesitate to cut off her hand."

### (5) The right to protection of dignity
- Protection from actions that damage one's reputation and dignity, and from **groundless accusations**.
- **(49:12) – meaning:** Avoid much **suspicion**; some suspicion is sin; **do not spy**; and do not **backbite** one another.

### (6) The right to protection of property
- The right to obtain the basic needs of life.
- **(4:32) – meaning:** **Men have a share of what they earn**, and **women have a share of what they earn**.

### (7) The right to protection of religious feelings
- **(6:108) – meaning:** **Do not insult** those they call upon besides Allah, lest they **insult Allah in hostility**, without knowledge. Thus We have made the deeds of every people seem fair to them.

| Right | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Life | 6:151, 5:32 |
| Equality | Hadith in Bayhaqi |
| Individual freedom | Hadith in Bukhari, 2:256 |
| Justice | 5:8, the Fatimah hadith |
| Dignity | 49:12 |
| Property | 4:32 |
| Religious feelings | 6:108 |

## ✅ Quick revision
- Only Allah has the right to lay down rights; rights in Islam = birthrights, permanent.
- Rights made by human beings: change with time, can be taken away, exist only on paper.
- The Charter of Madinah (start of the hijrah) → six centuries later the Magna Carta (1215, King John, England).
- Dr C. G. Weeramantry – "Islamic Jurisprudence: An International Perspective".
- 7 rights: life, equality, freedom, justice, dignity, property, religious feelings.
