### A1. 2016 – Islam I, Q23

Question ID: P2016-I-Q23  
Year: 2016  
Paper and question: Islam I, Q23  
Marks: not stated on the paper (Islam I multiple-choice question)  
Source: OL_2016_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

What a person must do for other human beings

- **A.** haqq Allah
- **B.** haqq an-nafs
- **C.** haqq al-'ibad
- **D.** haqq al-jar

**Correct answer:** C – haqq al-'ibad *(option (3) on the original paper)*

**Explanation:** Haqq al-'ibad = the duties owed to (Allah's) servants, i.e. other people. Haqq Allah – duty to Allah; haqq al-jar – duty to neighbours only.

### B1. 2016 – Islam II, Q4 (ii)

Question ID: P2016-II-Q4.1  
Year: 2016  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q4 (ii)  
Marks: 10 marks for the whole of Q4  
Source: OL_2016_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(ii) Point out the distinctive features of the human rights granted by Islam.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Given by Allah – birthrights drawn from the Quran and the Sunnah.
2. Permanent – they do not change with time or place.
3. No one can take them away; denying them is not only illegal but also a sin that deserves punishment.
4. They are put into practice continuously, not just on paper.
5. They suit human nature; they are equal for everyone without discrimination of race, colour or country.
6. They were proclaimed through the Charter of Madinah six centuries before the Western world.

**Answer explanation:** Comparing them with man-made rights strengthens the answer.

**Marking guidance (suggested only – not an official marking scheme):** No official marking scheme.

**Answer status:** Suggested – not an official answer (no official marking scheme was supplied); based on the Grade 10/11 textbook content

### B3. 2021 (2022) – Islam II, Q7 (iii)

Question ID: P2021_2022-II-Q7.3  
Year: 2021 (2022)  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q7 (iii)  
Marks: 10 marks (short notes on two topics)  
Source: OL_2021_2022_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(iii) The human rights granted by Islam

**Suggested answer:**

- The right to lay down rights belongs to Allah; they are birthrights – permanent and cannot be taken away (man-made rights change with time and are only on paper).
- The Charter of Madinah – six centuries before Magna Carta (1215).
- Seven rights:
  1. The right to life (6:151; 5:32)
  2. The right to equality – no difference between Arab and non-Arab, white and black (Bayhaqi)
  3. Personal freedom – the condemnation of selling free people as slaves (Bukhari); no compulsion in religion (2:256)
  4. The right to justice (5:8; "even if Fatimah stole…")
  5. The right to protection of dignity (49:12)
  6. The right to property (4:32)
  7. Protection of religious feelings (6:108)
- The view of Dr C. Weeramantry: a declaration of human rights compiled from Islamic legal literature would be comparable to today's charter.

**Answer explanation:** Give one piece of evidence for each right.

**Marking guidance (suggested only – not an official marking scheme):** No official marking scheme.

**Answer status:** Suggested – not an official answer (no official marking scheme was supplied); based on the Grade 10/11 textbook content
