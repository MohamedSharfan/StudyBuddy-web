When the Prophet ﷺ, while performing Hajj, asked Ibn 'Abbas (RA) to gather stones for him to throw at the miqats (i.e. the jamarat), the stones he brought were

- **A.** small stones
- **B.** large stones
- **C.** rocks
- **D.** pebbles

### B1. 2016 – Islam II, Q7 (iv)

Question ID: P2016-II-Q7.4  
Year: 2016  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q7 (iv)  
Marks: 10 marks (short notes on two topics)  
Source: OL_2016_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(iv) Keeping to the middle way (wasatiyyah)

**Suggested answer:**

Wasatiyyah = taking the balanced position between two extremes. The Muslim community is "ummatan wasatan" – a balanced community (2:143).
- In worship: the incidents of 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr (RA) and the three Companions – "Whoever does not follow my Sunnah is not of me" (Bukhari); Mu'adh (RA) – "Make things easy for people" (Muslim).
- The warning against going to extremes in religion (Ahmad).
- Balance in spending (17:29), in food (7:31), and in walking and speech (31:19).
- Asking for the good of both this world and the Hereafter (2:201).

**Answer explanation:** Definition + balance in several areas of life, with evidence.

### B2. 2020 – Islam II, Q6 (iii)

Question ID: P2020-II-Q6.3  
Year: 2020  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q6 (iii)  
Marks: 10 marks for the whole of Q6  
Source: OL_2020_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(iii) Clarify five ways Islam has put forward concerning keeping to the middle way (wasatiyyah) in life.

**Suggested answer:**

1. Balance in worship – 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr (RA): "Your body, your wife and your guests have rights over you" (Bukhari); the incident of the three Companions.
2. Not going to extremes in religion – "Beware of going to extremes in religion" (Ahmad); Mu'adh (RA) – "Make things easy for people" (Muslim).
3. Balance in spending – neither miserliness nor extravagance (17:29).
4. Balance in food and drink – eat and drink, but do not waste (7:31).
5. Balance in walking and speech – a moderate pace, a lowered voice (31:19).
6. Balance between this world and the Hereafter – asking for good in both worlds (2:201); caring for both body and soul.

**Answer explanation:** As the question says "clarify", give evidence / an example for each way.

**Marking guidance (suggested only – not an official marking scheme):** No official marking scheme.

**Question:**

(i) Write the meaning of 'wasatiyyah', with a definition.

**Suggested answer:**

Wasatiyyah (linguistically: the middle) = the balanced way that keeps balance and justice in all aspects of life, between going to excess (ifrat) and falling short (tafrit). "Thus We have made you a balanced community" (2:143, meaning).

### B4. 2024 (2025) – Islam II, Q2 (iii)

Question ID: P2024_2025-II-Q2.3  
Year: 2024 (2025)  
Paper and question: Islam II, Q2 (iii)  
Marks: 10 marks for the whole of Q2  
Source: OL_2024_2025_Islam_Tamil_Medium.md (page number not identifiable in the Markdown source)

**Question:**

(iii) Explain one incident from the life of the Prophet ﷺ that stresses the need to keep to the middle way in life.

**Suggested answer:**

Any one of the following:
1. The incident of the three Companions (Bukhari): one said he would pray all night, another that he would fast all year, the third that he would never marry. The Prophet ﷺ: "I fear Allah more than you; yet I fast and I leave fasting; I pray and I sleep; and I marry women; whoever does not follow my Sunnah is not of me."
2. The incident of 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr (RA) (Bukhari): continuous fasting and praying all night – "Your body, your wife and your guests have rights over you."
3. When Mu'adh (RA) led the prayer reciting long surahs – "Make things easy for people" (Muslim).
4. When Ibn 'Abbas (RA) brought small stones, the Prophet warned against going to extremes in religion (Ahmad).

**Answer explanation:** Explain the incident and the lesson drawn from it.
