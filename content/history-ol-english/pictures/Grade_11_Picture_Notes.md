# Chapter 02 – Establishment of British Power in Sri Lanka

### 19. Chapter_02_Figure_01  (Figure)

![Fig 2.1 – Dutch Stadtholder William V](Assets/Chapter_02_Figure_01.png)

**What it shows:** Dutch Stadtholder William V

**Section:** 2.3 Establishment of British Power – three stages

**Info notes:**

- After the Portuguese and Dutch held the coast, the British became the next power. This chapter covers how they established power and the struggles of the first half of the 19th century.
- East India Company: December 1600, royal charter from Queen Elizabeth I. First ships went to Sumatra; William Hawkins negotiated trade with Mughal emperor Jahangir.
- 1795 – Robert Andrews – Rajadhirajasinghe – King agreed to help; English later refused to share gains after taking Dutch forts
- French Revolution (1789) → French invaded Holland (1794); Stadtholder William V fled to Britain.
- Kew Letter (from Kew Palace): instructed the Dutch to admit British troops; Dutch governor Van Angelbeek (Funn Englebeck) received it in Feb 1795 but refused. British attacked Trincomalee (1795); then Batticaloa, Mullaitivu, Point Pedro, Ja
- Meuron Commission (Brigadier De Meuron) recommended: local officers instead of Madras collectors; abolishing new taxes including coconut tax; separate judicial and revenue officers; restore the Dutch court system.
