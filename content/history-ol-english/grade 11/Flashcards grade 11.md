# StudyBuddy – History Grade 11 – Flashcards (English Medium)

> Each card has a **Front** (question) and a **Back** (answer). Cover the back, answer aloud, then check.


## Chapter 01 – Industrial Revolution

**Card 01**
**Front:** Define the Industrial Revolution.
**Back:** The broad change over about six decades in which machines replaced manual labour in production.

**Card 02**
**Front:** Where and when did it begin?
**Back:** Britain, late 18th century.

**Card 03**
**Front:** Why did Britain have a wealthy investor class?
**Back:** Its trade and finance had surpassed Holland, France, Portugal and Spain.

**Card 04**
**Front:** What was the "encroaching (enclosure) movement"?
**Back:** Rich landowners merged small farms into large farms; displaced farmers became factory labour.

**Card 05**
**Front:** How did colonies help British industry?
**Back:** Commercial laws forced colonies to buy British goods, ensuring high demand.

**Card 06**
**Front:** Name the three base industries.
**Back:** Textile, iron and steel, and coal.

**Card 07**
**Front:** Who invented the flying shuttle and when?
**Back:** John Kay, 1733.

**Card 08**
**Front:** Who invented the Spinning Jenny?
**Back:** James Hargreaves, 1764.

**Card 09**
**Front:** What was Arkwright's invention?
**Back:** A water-powered spinning machine (water frame), 1769.

**Card 10**
**Front:** What did Samuel Crompton create?
**Back:** The "Mule" (c. 1779) for finer, stronger thread.

**Card 11**
**Front:** Who applied steam power to the mule?
**Back:** Edmund Cartwright (c. 1785).

**Card 12**
**Front:** Who first smelted iron with coal?
**Back:** Abraham Darby, 1730.

**Card 13**
**Front:** Who invented the rolling process for wrought iron?
**Back:** Henry Cort, 1784.

**Card 14**
**Front:** What did Henry Bessemer do?
**Back:** Made cheap steel by removing impurities from iron (1856).

**Card 15**
**Front:** What did Wilhelm Siemens introduce?
**Back:** The electric furnace for smelting (1870).

**Card 16**
**Front:** Where were British coal mines?
**Back:** South Wales, Yorkshire and Lancashire.

**Card 17**
**Front:** Who invented the first steam engine for mines?
**Back:** Thomas Newcomen, 1735.

**Card 18**
**Front:** Who improved the steam engine?
**Back:** James Watt.

**Card 19**
**Front:** What did Humphry Davy invent?
**Back:** The miners' safety lamp (1812).

**Card 20**
**Front:** What were coal by-products?
**Back:** Tar, dyes, medicines, perfumes, oil and fertilizers.

**Card 21**
**Front:** What is the Macadam method?
**Back:** Road-building with layers of crushed granite on a base of large stones, pounded flat (John McAdam, 1811).

**Card 22**
**Front:** Who built the first successful locomotive?
**Back:** George Stephenson, 1814.

**Card 23**
**Front:** Who built the steamboat and the passenger steamship?
**Back:** Robert Fulton (1807); Henry Bell (1811).

**Card 24**
**Front:** Who built the motor car and the aeroplane?
**Back:** Daimler (1885); Wright brothers (1903).

**Card 25**
**Front:** What was the Penny Post?
**Back:** A cheap postal service begun in 1840.

**Card 26**
**Front:** Who invented the telegraph and telephone?
**Back:** Samuel Morse (1844); Alexander Graham Bell (1876).

**Card 27**
**Front:** Who invented radio and television?
**Back:** Marconi (1895); John Logie Baird.

**Card 28**
**Front:** What did Jethro Tull introduce?
**Back:** Line sowing with a horse-drawn seed drill.

**Card 29**
**Front:** What did Robert Bakewell do?
**Back:** Improved sheep breeding.

**Card 30**
**Front:** What is guano fertilizer?
**Back:** The first manure of the industrial age, produced in Africa from 1800.

**Card 31**
**Front:** Why did it spread to other countries?
**Back:** Growing demand, better transport and coal in France and Germany.

**Card 32**
**Front:** Which Asian country industrialized rapidly late in the 19th century?
**Back:** Japan.

**Card 33**
**Front:** What does "laissez-faire" mean?
**Back:** Minimal government involvement in the economy (Adam Smith).

**Card 34**
**Front:** What is neo-imperialism?
**Back:** Competition for colonies after 1850; a cause of World War I.

**Card 35**
**Front:** Name two political ideas from the working-class struggle.
**Back:** Socialism and liberalism.

**Card 36**
**Front:** What effects did factories have on cities?
**Back:** Overcrowding, slums and poor sanitation (e.g., Vienna 1847).

**Card 37**
**Front:** What were factory conditions?
**Back:** 12–14-hour days; women and children employed without protection.

**Card 38**
**Front:** What environmental problem arose?
**Back:** Water and air pollution.

**Card 39**
**Front:** How did the industrial revolution affect Sri Lanka?
**Back:** Plantations (tea, coconut, rubber), factories, new classes, banks, roads, railways, post.

**Card 40**
**Front:** Who started the coffee-pounding mill in 1804?
**Back:** Governor Frederick North.

**Card 41**
**Front:** Which governor built the Colombo–Kandy road?
**Back:** Edward Barnes.

**Card 42**
**Front:** When was the Colombo–Kandy train service started?
**Back:** August 1867 (Kandy line begun 1858, completed 1863).

**Card 43**
**Front:** When did the railway reach Nawalapitiya and Bandarawela?
**Back:** 1871 and 1899.

**Card 44**
**Front:** When was Sri Lanka's first postage stamp issued?
**Back:** 1857; telegram service began 1858.


## Chapter 02 – Establishment of British Power in Sri Lanka

**Card 01**
**Front:** When and by whom was the English East India Company chartered?
**Back:** December 1600, by Queen Elizabeth I.

**Card 02**
**Front:** Where did the first Company ships go?
**Back:** Sumatra; later India (William Hawkins met Emperor Jahangir).

**Card 03**
**Front:** What was the Ambon massacre?
**Back:** The 1623 Dutch attack killing English traders, after which the English left South-East Asia.

**Card 04**
**Front:** Name three early English trade centres in India.
**Back:** Bombay, Calcutta and Madras.

**Card 05**
**Front:** Which French centres existed in India?
**Back:** Pondicherry, Karikal and Chandernagore.

**Card 06**
**Front:** Why did the English ignore Sri Lanka at first?
**Back:** They were not yet strong in India and the Dutch had strong forts.

**Card 07**
**Front:** Give the two reasons for British interest in Sri Lanka.
**Back:** Its location near India and the Bay of Bengal, and cinnamon and other trade goods.

**Card 08**
**Front:** Why was Trincomalee valuable?
**Back:** It faces the Bay of Bengal, protects ships from the north-east monsoon and guards India's eastern coast.

**Card 09**
**Front:** Name the three British envoys to Kandy and years.
**Back:** John Pybus (1762), Hugh Boyd (1782), Robert Andrews (1795).

**Card 10**
**Front:** Why did the first two missions fail?
**Back:** English and Dutch cooperated; the English would not help against the Dutch.

**Card 11**
**Front:** Name the three stages of British conquest.
**Back:** Coast (1796), Crown colony (1802), Kandy (1815).

**Card 12**
**Front:** What was the Kew Letter?
**Back:** A letter from Stadtholder William V ordering the Dutch to admit British troops to stop the French.

**Card 13**
**Front:** Who was the Dutch governor in 1795?
**Back:** Van Angelbeek (Funn Englebeck).

**Card 14**
**Front:** Which fort fell first in 1795?
**Back:** Trincomalee.

**Card 15**
**Front:** What treaty was signed on 16 February 1796?
**Back:** Dutch surrender of Colombo and the coast; Dutch left peacefully.

**Card 16**
**Front:** Who governed the coast 1796–1798?
**Back:** The Company through Madras, with a Resident, Collectors and Avumildars.

**Card 17**
**Front:** What was a Kachcheri?
**Back:** The collector's office, which began the Kachcheri system.

**Card 18**
**Front:** Which unpopular tax caused the 1797 rebellion?
**Back:** The coconut-tree tax.

**Card 19**
**Front:** What did the Meuron Commission recommend?
**Back:** Local officers, abolishing new taxes, separate judicial and revenue staff, Dutch court system.

**Card 20**
**Front:** What was "dual administration"?
**Back:** Governor (Crown) and Company shared rule 1798–1802; it failed.

**Card 21**
**Front:** Who was the first governor?
**Back:** Frederick North (1798).

**Card 22**
**Front:** When did the coast become a Crown colony?
**Back:** 1802.

**Card 23**
**Front:** Who enthroned Sri Wickrama Rajasinghe?
**Back:** Maha Adikaram Pilimathalawe in 1798.

**Card 24**
**Front:** What was the MacDowell mission (1800)?
**Back:** A British embassy with 1164 soldiers; the king refused the army and the mission failed.

**Card 25**
**Front:** Why did the British want Kandy?
**Back:** Security, cost, rebel support, French threat, and a road link to Trincomalee.

**Card 26**
**Front:** What happened in 1803?
**Back:** British invaded Kandy, installed Muththusami, were defeated by the Kandyans, rain and disease.

**Card 27**
**Front:** Who was John D'Oyly?
**Back:** British civil servant who studied local culture, used spies and won over Kandyan chiefs.

**Card 28**
**Front:** Which governors prepared and carried out the 1815 invasion?
**Back:** Thomas Maitland prepared; Robert Brownrigg invaded.

**Card 29**
**Front:** Name reasons the Kandyan kingdom fell.
**Back:** King's quarrel with chiefs and monks, cruelty (Ehelepola's family), forced labour, British strategy.

**Card 30**
**Front:** Where was the king captured and when?
**Back:** Medamahanuwara, 18 February 1815.

**Card 31**
**Front:** What was the Kandyan Convention?
**Back:** 2 March 1815 treaty at Magul Maduwa giving the kingdom to Britain and protecting Buddhism.

**Card 32**
**Front:** Which monk is said to have raised the Sinhala flag?
**Back:** Wariyapola Sri Sumangala Thero.

**Card 33**
**Front:** What did clause 5 promise?
**Back:** Protection of Buddhism, devales, monks and temples.

**Card 34**
**Front:** Who was Resident in Kandy?
**Back:** John D'Oyly.

**Card 35**
**Front:** What years was the "1818 struggle"?
**Back:** September 1817 – October 1818.

**Card 36**
**Front:** Name causes of the 1818 rebellion.
**Back:** Loss of king, threat to religion and culture, loss of chiefs' status and income.

**Card 37**
**Front:** Who was Doresami?
**Back:** A pretender to the throne who led the Wellassa rebellion.

**Card 38**
**Front:** What did Keppetipola do?
**Back:** Sent to suppress rebels but joined them; became Maha Adikaram.

**Card 39**
**Front:** Which chiefs stayed loyal to the British?
**Back:** Molligoda and Eknaligoda.

**Card 40**
**Front:** What did the British do to suppress it?
**Back:** Martial law, burning villages, mass arrests.

**Card 41**
**Front:** When were Keppetipola and Madugalle executed?
**Back:** 26 November 1818.

**Card 42**
**Front:** What was the 21 November 1818 Proclamation?
**Back:** A 56-clause document curtailing chiefs' power and introducing English law.

**Card 43**
**Front:** What were the causes of the 1848 rebellion?
**Back:** Colebrooke reforms, land laws (1840) and Torrington's new taxes.

**Card 44**
**Front:** Name five 1848 taxes.
**Back:** Stamp, cart/boat, shop, gun, body (poll) and dog taxes.

**Card 45**
**Front:** Who were the 1848 leaders?
**Back:** Gongalegoda Banda, Puran Appu, Dingirala and Diyes.

**Card 46**
**Front:** Where was Gongalegoda Banda crowned?
**Back:** Dambulla temple.

**Card 47**
**Front:** What happened to Puran Appu and Kadahapola Thero?
**Back:** Puran Appu was executed; the thero was shot after a brief trial.

**Card 48**
**Front:** What followed in Britain?
**Back:** Torrington was recalled; shop and dog taxes abolished.


## Chapter 03 – National Renaissance in Sri Lanka

**Card 01**
**Front:** What are the two phases of the independence movement after 1850?
**Back:** National and religious renaissance (1850–1915) and political agitation (1915–1948).

**Card 02**
**Front:** Why did Western culture spread widely after 1815?
**Back:** The Kandyan kingdom no longer protected the interior.

**Card 03**
**Front:** Name the missionary organizations and their arrival years.
**Back:** London (1804), Baptist (1812), Wesleyan (1814), American (1816), Church Missionary (1818).

**Card 04**
**Front:** Which methods did missionaries use?
**Back:** Writing, preaching and education.

**Card 05**
**Front:** Why did natives attend mission schools?
**Back:** English was needed for government jobs.

**Card 06**
**Front:** Which missionary publications are named?
**Back:** Masika Thegga (1832), Lanka Nidhanaya (1840).

**Card 07**
**Front:** What two problems did Buddhists face?
**Back:** Missionary criticism of their religion and lack of support for native-language education.

**Card 08**
**Front:** Who founded the Parama Dhamma Chethiya Pirivena?
**Back:** Walane Siddhartha Thero, Ratmalana.

**Card 09**
**Front:** Who founded Vidyodaya Pirivena and when?
**Back:** Hikkaduwe Sri Sumangala Thero, 1873, Maligakanda.

**Card 10**
**Front:** Who founded Vidyalankara Pirivena?
**Back:** Ratmalane Dhammaloka Thero, 1875, Peliyagoda.

**Card 11**
**Front:** Who set up the Lankopakara Press?
**Back:** Bulathgama Siri Sumanatissa Thero, Galle, 1862, helped by the King of Siam.

**Card 12**
**Front:** Name Buddhist newspapers.
**Back:** Lakmini Pahana, Lankaloka, Sarasavi Sandaresa, Sinhala Jathiya, Sinhala Bauddhaya.

**Card 13**
**Front:** Who led the Buddhist debates?
**Back:** Migettuwatte Gunananda Thero.

**Card 14**
**Front:** List the Five Great Debates.
**Back:** Baddegama 1865, Waragoda 1865, Udanwita 1866, Gampola 1871, Panadura 1873.

**Card 15**
**Front:** Which debate was most famous?
**Back:** Panadura (1873).

**Card 16**
**Front:** Who came to Sri Lanka after reading about the Panadura debate?
**Back:** Henry Steel Olcott.

**Card 17**
**Front:** What did Olcott do?
**Back:** Became Buddhist and helped found the Colombo Parama Vignanartha movement (1880) and Buddhist schools.

**Card 18**
**Front:** Name Buddhist schools founded.
**Back:** Ananda, Dharmaraja, Mahinda, Vijaya, Jinaraja, Anuruddha.

**Card 19**
**Front:** Which two lasting symbols came from this movement?
**Back:** The Vesak poya holiday and the Buddhist flag.

**Card 20**
**Front:** Who was Anagarika Dharmapala?
**Back:** Buddhist leader who preached nationalism, edited Sinhala Bauddhaya and Mahabodhi, and worked on Buddha Gaya.

**Card 21**
**Front:** Which novelist criticised imitation of the West?
**Back:** Piyadasa Sirisena.

**Card 22**
**Front:** Who wrote the drama "Sinhala Parabhawa"?
**Back:** John de Silva (the Sura Act on alcohol).

**Card 23**
**Front:** Which European scholars studied ancient ruins?
**Back:** George Turner, Rhys Davids, H.C.P. Bell and Henry Parker.

**Card 24**
**Front:** Who protected Anuradhapura's sacred sites?
**Back:** Walisinghe Harischandra's movement.

**Card 25**
**Front:** Who led the Hindu renaissance?
**Back:** Arumuga Navalar.

**Card 26**
**Front:** What did Navalar do?
**Back:** Founded presses (Udayabhanu), wrote textbooks, translated Puranas and founded Saiva Prakasa Vidyalaya (1849).

**Card 27**
**Front:** What became of the Sivangala school?
**Back:** Jaffna Hindu College.

**Card 28**
**Front:** Which two leaders followed Navalar?
**Back:** Ponnambalam Ramanathan and Ponnambalam Arunachalam.

**Card 29**
**Front:** What did Ramanathan do in 1915?
**Back:** Defended the Buddhists against martial law.

**Card 30**
**Front:** What did Arunachalam found in 1919?
**Back:** The Ceylon National Congress.

**Card 31**
**Front:** Who led the Islamic renaissance?
**Back:** M.C. Siddi Lebbe.

**Card 32**
**Front:** What did he establish?
**Back:** Newspaper Muslim Nesan (1882), Zahira College and Muslim Academic Club.

**Card 33**
**Front:** Who was T.B. Jayah?
**Back:** Principal of Zahira College and a leader who founded Muslim schools.


## Chapter 04 – Political Changes in Sri Lanka under the British

**Card 01**
**Front:** How long did British rule over the whole island last?
**Back:** 133 years (1815–1948).

**Card 02**
**Front:** Who investigated administration and who the courts in 1829–30?
**Back:** W.M.G. Colebrooke (administration/finance) and Charles H. Cameron (judiciary).

**Card 03**
**Front:** When were the Colebrooke reforms introduced?
**Back:** 1833.

**Card 04**
**Front:** List the main features of the Colebrooke reforms.
**Back:** Executive and Legislative Councils, unified administration, five provinces, end of rajakariya, end of trade monopoly.

**Card 05**
**Front:** What was the 1833 Legislative Council composition?
**Back:** 15 members – 9 official, 6 unofficial (3 European, 1 Sinhalese, 1 Tamil, 1 Burgher).

**Card 06**
**Front:** What is communal (nationalist) representation?
**Back:** Appointing unofficial members by ethnic group.

**Card 07**
**Front:** Who sat on the Executive Council?
**Back:** Colonial Secretary, treasurer, auditor, surveyor-general, customs collector.

**Card 08**
**Front:** What was the Lanka Congress?
**Back:** A European planters' organization of 1865 led by George Wall.

**Card 09**
**Front:** Result of the European agitation?
**Back:** Unofficial members raised to 8 (Kandyan Sinhalese and Muslims added).

**Card 10**
**Front:** Who sent the first reform petition in 1908?
**Back:** James Pieris.

**Card 11**
**Front:** Who opposed and who approved the reforms?
**Back:** Governor McCallum opposed; Colonial Secretary Lord Crewe announced them in 1910.

**Card 12**
**Front:** Crewe–McCallum Council composition?
**Back:** 21 members – 11 official, 10 unofficial (6 nominated, 4 elected).

**Card 13**
**Front:** What was new in 1910?
**Back:** First limited franchise for elected members; a seat for educated Ceylonese.

**Card 14**
**Front:** Who was elected as educated Ceylonese in 1912?
**Back:** Ponnambalam Ramanathan.

**Card 15**
**Front:** Why did the 1912 temperance protests start?
**Back:** The new Excise Ordinance allowed more toddy taverns.

**Card 16**
**Front:** What happened in 1915?
**Back:** Sinhalese–Muslim riots; government used martial law and arrested temperance leaders.

**Card 17**
**Front:** Which leaders emerged from the temperance movement?
**Back:** F.R. Senanayake, D.S. Senanayake and D.B. Jayatilaka.

**Card 18**
**Front:** What was "Swaraj"?
**Back:** The Indian demand for self-rule.

**Card 19**
**Front:** When did Gandhi visit Sri Lanka?
**Back:** 1927.

**Card 20**
**Front:** When was the Ceylon National Congress formed and by whom first led?
**Back:** December 1919; Ponnambalam Arunachalam.

**Card 21**
**Front:** What was the 1920 Manning council like?
**Back:** 37 members – 14 official, 23 unofficial (7 nominated, 16 elected).

**Card 22**
**Front:** First unofficial majority happened when?
**Back:** 1920.

**Card 23**
**Front:** Manning–Devonshire (1924) numbers?
**Back:** 49 members – 12 official, 37 unofficial (8 nominated, 29 elected).

**Card 24**
**Front:** What was its problem?
**Back:** An elected majority had power but the governor held responsibility.

**Card 25**
**Front:** Who headed the commission that led to the 1931 constitution?
**Back:** Lord Donoughmore.

**Card 26**
**Front:** How was the State Council composed?
**Back:** 61 members: 50 elected, 8 nominated and 3 Officers of State.

**Card 27**
**Front:** What is universal franchise?
**Back:** Voting rights for all men and women over 21 regardless of wealth (1931).

**Card 28**
**Front:** What percentage had franchise in 1924?
**Back:** About 4 %.

**Card 29**
**Front:** What was the Executive Committee system?
**Back:** The State Council formed 7 committees whose chairmen became ministers.

**Card 30**
**Front:** Two projects under the State Council.
**Back:** Agricultural colonies and free education.

**Card 31**
**Front:** Which subjects stayed with Officers of State?
**Back:** Defence, finance and legal affairs.

**Card 32**
**Front:** Who led the Suriyamal movement?
**Back:** N.M. Perera and Colvin R. de Silva of the Lanka Sama Samaja Party.

**Card 33**
**Front:** What did poppy and sunflower symbolise?
**Back:** Poppy – loyalty to the Empire; sunflower – independence.

**Card 34**
**Front:** What did the ministers demand during WWII?
**Back:** A new constitution after the war in return for their support.

**Card 35**
**Front:** When was the Soulbury report issued and approved?
**Back:** Report 1945; State Council approved November 1945.


## Chapter 05 – Social Changes in Sri Lanka under the British

**Card 01**
**Front:** What characterised the traditional village economy?
**Back:** Self-sufficiency, mutual help (Aththama), minimal money and no wage labour.

**Card 02**
**Front:** What did the Gamsabha do?
**Back:** Settled minor disputes and organised community works such as tanks and roads.

**Card 03**
**Front:** What was rajakariya?
**Back:** Compulsory unpaid service to the king for a set number of days.

**Card 04**
**Front:** Why did Colebrooke encourage the sale of land?
**Back:** To let private (local and foreign) entrepreneurs invest.

**Card 05**
**Front:** What was the effect of abolishing rajakariya?
**Back:** Labour became purchasable – a wage-earning class began.

**Card 06**
**Front:** Which monopolies were ended in 1833?
**Back:** Cinnamon and salt.

**Card 07**
**Front:** What replaced the fish tax in kind?
**Back:** A permit system for boats.

**Card 08**
**Front:** Why a savings bank?
**Back:** To give planters money at reasonable interest.

**Card 09**
**Front:** Why did foreigners dominate plantations?
**Back:** Few locals had capital.

**Card 10**
**Front:** Which factors helped coffee?
**Back:** Colebrooke reforms, European demand, high profit, cheap Indian labour, lower export tax.

**Card 11**
**Front:** What did Edward Barnes do for coffee?
**Back:** Cut the export tax and freed coffee workers from compulsory service.

**Card 12**
**Front:** Which regions grew coffee?
**Back:** Dumbara, Gampola, Peradeniya, Matale, Badulla and the Central Province.

**Card 13**
**Front:** What hit coffee in 1844?
**Back:** Britain's free-trade policy and a world market slump.

**Card 14**
**Front:** What destroyed coffee?
**Back:** The leaf disease Hemileia vastatrix from 1869.

**Card 15**
**Front:** Why did cinchona and cocoa fail as replacements?
**Back:** Limited demand and altitude limits; cocoa needed shade and stayed under 1200 acres.

**Card 16**
**Front:** Who experimented with tea at Loolecondera?
**Back:** James Taylor.

**Card 17**
**Front:** Why were estate workers sent to Assam in 1867?
**Back:** To study tea cultivation.

**Card 18**
**Front:** How much tea was grown in 1875 and 1894?
**Back:** About 500 acres; about 400 000 acres.

**Card 19**
**Front:** Where could tea grow?
**Back:** Everywhere except the dry zone; Central, Uva, Sabaragamuwa first.

**Card 20**
**Front:** Why did tea succeed?
**Back:** World demand, good climate, machinery, roads and cheap South Indian labour.

**Card 21**
**Front:** What was special about the coconut plantation?
**Back:** Local ownership – 65 % by 1880; half the crop used locally.

**Card 22**
**Front:** Name coconut-growing districts.
**Back:** Kurunegala, Chilaw, Gampaha.

**Card 23**
**Front:** Where is the Coconut Research Institute?
**Back:** Lunuwila.

**Card 24**
**Front:** When and why did rubber expand?
**Back:** After 1900: motor-vehicle demand, new varieties, falling tea prices.

**Card 25**
**Front:** Where is the Rubber Research Institute?
**Back:** Agalawatta.

**Card 26**
**Front:** What share of exports did coconut and rubber give in 1920?
**Back:** Coconut 27 %; rubber 30 %.

**Card 27**
**Front:** Who were the "elite" aristocrats?
**Back:** Traditional landowning leaders with high status and government posts.

**Card 28**
**Front:** How did the new middle class earn money?
**Back:** Contracting, arrack renting, graphite, trade, transport and coconut estates.

**Card 29**
**Front:** What jobs did English education open?
**Back:** Lawyers, engineers, surveyors, clerks and civil servants.

**Card 30**
**Front:** Who worked in the plantations?
**Back:** South Indian Tamil labourers.

**Card 31**
**Front:** Name early 20th-century strikes.
**Back:** Printers, Colombo launderers and carters.

**Card 32**
**Front:** Who led urban workers 1922–1935?
**Back:** A.E. Goonesinha.

**Card 33**
**Front:** Who organised estate-worker unions?
**Back:** Natesa Aiyar.

**Card 34**
**Front:** List cultural changes.
**Back:** English education, Western dress, food, customs, architecture and clubs.

**Card 35**
**Front:** Give three advantages of British rule.
**Back:** English language, better transport/communication, export plantations and a parliamentary system.

**Card 36**
**Front:** Give three disadvantages.
**Back:** Exploitation and poverty, decline of indigenous culture, landlessness and destruction of Gamsabha.


## Chapter 06 – Receiving of Independence to Sri Lanka

**Card 01**
**Front:** When did the Soulbury Commission arrive and report?
**Back:** Arrived December 1944; report September 1945.

**Card 02**
**Front:** Who persuaded Britain to promise independence before the 1947 election?
**Back:** D.S. Senanayake and other leaders.

**Card 03**
**Front:** Who founded the United National Party and when?
**Back:** D.S. Senanayake, 1946.

**Card 04**
**Front:** How many seats were in the 1947 parliament and how many did the UNP win?
**Back:** 95 seats; the UNP won 42.

**Card 05**
**Front:** What were the November 1947 agreements?
**Back:** A defence agreement (British bases) and an external affairs agreement.

**Card 06**
**Front:** When was independence granted?
**Back:** 4 February 1948 (Order in Council 19 December 1947).

**Card 07**
**Front:** Who hoisted the Sri Lankan flag at the opening of Parliament?
**Back:** D.S. Senanayake, on 10 February 1948.

**Card 08**
**Front:** What was the Governor-General's role?
**Back:** Nominal representative of the Queen who acted on the PM's advice.

**Card 09**
**Front:** Composition of the Soulbury Parliament?
**Back:** House of Representatives 101 (95 elected + 6 nominated) and Senate 30; term 5 years.

**Card 10**
**Front:** Who held defence and external affairs?
**Back:** The Prime Minister.

**Card 11**
**Front:** What is the Westminster principle in the cabinet?
**Back:** Collective responsibility to Parliament.

**Card 12**
**Front:** Difference between dual-party and multi-party systems?
**Back:** Two major parties (UK, USA) vs several parties (France, India, Sri Lanka).

**Card 13**
**Front:** Who founded the LSSP and when?
**Back:** N.M. Perera, Colvin R. de Silva, Philip Gunawardena, 1935.

**Card 14**
**Front:** Which party split from LSSP in 1943?
**Back:** The Communist Party under S.A. Wickramasinghe.

**Card 15**
**Front:** Who founded the SLFP and when?
**Back:** S.W.R.D. Bandaranaike, 1951.

**Card 16**
**Front:** What was the MEP?
**Back:** Mahajana Eksath Peramuna, the 1956 alliance led by SLFP.

**Card 17**
**Front:** Who led Tamil Congress and Federal Party?
**Back:** G.G. Ponnambalam; S.J.V. Chelvanayakam (1949).

**Card 18**
