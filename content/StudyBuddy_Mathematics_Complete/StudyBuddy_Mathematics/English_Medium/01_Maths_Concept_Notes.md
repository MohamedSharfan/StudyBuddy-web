## G10C01 · Grade 10 · Chapter 1 — Perimeter

<!-- meta {"id": "G10C01", "grade": 10, "chapter": 1, "area": "MEN", "sections": ["P1A", "P1B"], "medium": "en"} -->

**Exam sections:** Paper I — Part A, Paper I — Part B

Perimeter is the total length around a shape. For circles and sectors we use π.

**Prerequisite:** Grade 9: circumference of a circle; adding lengths with units.

### Key ideas

- Circumference of a circle: $2\pi r$ (or $\pi d$).
- Arc length of a sector: $2\pi r\times\frac{\theta}{360}$.
- Perimeter of a sector = arc + 2 radii.
- For composite shapes, trace the OUTSIDE edge only.

**Formula:** `F-MEN-ARC` $l = 2\pi r\times\frac{\theta}{360}$, `F-MEN-CIRC` $C = 2\pi r = \pi d$

**Diagram:** `GEO-SECTOR-01` `Shared_Assets/Geometry/GEO-SECTOR-01.svg`, `GEO-COMPOSITE-01` `Shared_Assets/Geometry/GEO-COMPOSITE-01.svg`

### Worked example (Foundation Mastery) — MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C01", "area": "MEN", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Foundation Mastery", "difficulty": "easy", "formula": "F-MEN-ARC", "diagram": "GEO-SECTOR-01", "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S1", "MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S2", "MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S3", "MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S4"]} -->
**Topic:** Perimeter · **Pathway:** Foundation Mastery · **Difficulty:** easy · **Formula:** `F-MEN-ARC`

**Question:** A sector has radius 7 cm and centre angle 90°. Find its perimeter. (Take π = 22/7.)

**Diagram:** `GEO-SECTOR-01` → `Shared_Assets/Geometry/GEO-SECTOR-01.svg` (generic diagram — not drawn to scale; use the values in the question)

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Write the arc length formula. <!-- MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S1 -->

$$l = 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta}{360}$$

Step 2 — Substitute r and θ. <!-- MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S2 -->

$$l = 2\times\frac{22}{7}\times 7\times\frac{90}{360}$$

Step 3 — Simplify the arc length. <!-- MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S3 -->

$$l = 11\ \text{cm}$$

Step 4 — Perimeter = arc + two radii. <!-- MQ-G10C01-F-SECTOR_PER-01-S4 -->

$$P = 11 + 7 + 7 = 25\ \text{cm}$$

**Final answer:** $25$ cm
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $25$ · **Unit:** cm

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Forgetting to add the two radii.

**Question:** A shape is a rectangle 20 cm long and 14 cm wide, with a semicircle drawn outward on one 14 cm side (that side is removed). Find the perimeter of the shape. (π = 22/7)

**Diagram:** `GEO-COMPOSITE-01` → `Shared_Assets/Geometry/GEO-COMPOSITE-01.svg` (generic diagram — not drawn to scale; use the values in the question)

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Radius of the semicircle = half of the width. <!-- MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S1 -->

$$r = 14\div 2 = 7\ \text{cm}$$

$$20+20+14 = 54\ \text{cm}$$

Step 4 — Total perimeter. <!-- MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S4 -->

$$P = 54 + 22 = 76\ \text{cm}$$

**Final answer:** $76$ cm
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $76$ · **Unit:** cm

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Including the hidden side under the semicircle.

## G10C02 · Grade 10 · Chapter 2 — Square Root

<!-- meta {"id": "G10C02", "grade": 10, "chapter": 2, "area": "NUM", "sections": ["P1A"], "medium": "en"} -->

**Exam sections:** Paper I — Part A

A square root of a number n is a number whose square is n. For non-squares we find an approximation.

**Prerequisite:** Squares of 1–20; prime factorisation.

### Key ideas

- Perfect squares: use prime factors in pairs.
- First approximation = square root correct to 1 decimal place.
- Locate between two whole numbers, then test decimals by squaring.
- $\sqrt{0.09} = 0.3$: the root has half the decimal places.

**Formula:** `F-NUM-SQRT` $\sqrt{n}\times\sqrt{n} = n$

### Worked example (Foundation Mastery) — MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C02", "area": "NUM", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Foundation Mastery", "difficulty": "easy", "formula": "F-NUM-SQRT", "diagram": null, "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01-S1", "MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01-S2", "MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01-S3"]} -->
**Topic:** Square Root · **Pathway:** Foundation Mastery · **Difficulty:** easy · **Formula:** `F-NUM-SQRT`

**Question:** Find √1296 using prime factors.

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Write the number as a product of prime factors, in pairs. <!-- MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01-S1 -->

$$1296 = (2\times 2)\times(2\times 2)\times(3\times 3)\times(3\times 3)$$

Step 2 — Take one factor from each pair. <!-- MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01-S2 -->

$$\sqrt{1296} = 2\times2\times3\times3$$

Step 3 — Multiply. <!-- MQ-G10C02-F-SQRT_PRIME-01-S3 -->

$$\sqrt{1296} = 36$$

**Final answer:** $36$
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $36$

### Worked example (Grade Improvement) — MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C02", "area": "NUM", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Grade Improvement", "difficulty": "medium", "formula": "F-NUM-SQRT", "diagram": null, "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S1", "MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S2", "MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S3", "MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S4"]} -->
**Topic:** Square Root · **Pathway:** Grade Improvement · **Difficulty:** medium · **Formula:** `F-NUM-SQRT`

**Question:** Find the first approximation (correct to the first decimal place) of √85.

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Find the perfect squares on either side. <!-- MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S1 -->

$$9^2=81 < 85 < 100=10^2 \Rightarrow 9 < \sqrt{85} < 10$$

Step 2 — Try the nearest one-decimal value by squaring. <!-- MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S2 -->

$$9.2^2 = 84.64$$

Step 3 — Check the half-way values. <!-- MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S3 -->

$$9.15^2 = 83.7225 < 85 < 85.5625 = 9.25^2$$

Step 4 — So the square root rounds to: <!-- MQ-G10C02-G-SQRT_FIRST-01-S4 -->

$$\sqrt{85} \approx 9.2$$

**Final answer:** $9.2$
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $9.2$

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Choosing the decimal whose square is just below N without checking which is closer.

- Writing √0.0144 as 0.012.

- **Grade Improvement:** Use the half-way check (a.x5)².

## G10C03 · Grade 10 · Chapter 3 — Fractions

<!-- meta {"id": "G10C03", "grade": 10, "chapter": 3, "area": "NUM", "sections": ["P1A", "P1B"], "medium": "en"} -->

**Exam sections:** Paper I — Part A, Paper I — Part B

Fractions describe parts of a whole. Word problems often use 'a fraction of the remainder'.

**Prerequisite:** LCM of numbers; mixed numbers ↔ improper fractions.

### Key ideas

- Add/subtract: make denominators equal (LCM).
- Multiply: top × top, bottom × bottom. Divide: multiply by the reciprocal.
- 'Of the remainder' → first find what is left, then take the fraction.
- If (fraction) × whole = amount, then whole = amount ÷ fraction.

**Formula:** `F-NUM-FRAC` $\frac ab\pm\frac cd = \frac{ad\pm bc}{bd},\quad \frac ab\div\frac cd = \frac ab\times\frac dc$

### Worked example (Foundation Mastery) — MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C03", "area": "NUM", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Foundation Mastery", "difficulty": "easy", "formula": "F-NUM-FRAC", "diagram": null, "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S1", "MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S2", "MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S3", "MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S4"]} -->
**Topic:** Fractions · **Pathway:** Foundation Mastery · **Difficulty:** easy · **Formula:** `F-NUM-FRAC`

**Question:** Simplify: $2\tfrac{1}{3} + 1\tfrac{1}{6}$

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Change mixed numbers to improper fractions. <!-- MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S1 -->

$$2\tfrac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3},\quad 1\tfrac{1}{6} = \frac{7}{6}$$

Step 2 — Use the common denominator 6. <!-- MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S2 -->

$$\frac{7}{3} + \frac{7}{6} = \frac{14}{6} + \frac{7}{6}$$

Step 3 — Add the numerators. <!-- MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S3 -->

$$= \frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2}$$

Step 4 — Write as a mixed number. <!-- MQ-G10C03-F-ADD_MIXED-01-S4 -->

$$= 3\tfrac{1}{2}$$

**Final answer:** $3\tfrac{1}{2}$
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $3\tfrac{1}{2}$, $\frac{7}{2}$

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Adding denominators together (e.g. 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5 is wrong).

## G10C04 · Grade 10 · Chapter 4 — Binomial Expressions

<!-- meta {"id": "G10C04", "grade": 10, "chapter": 4, "area": "ALG", "sections": ["P1A"], "medium": "en"} -->

**Exam sections:** Paper I — Part A

Binomial expressions have two terms, like (x + 3). We expand products of binomials.

**Prerequisite:** Grade 9: expanding a(b + c); directed numbers.

### Key ideas

- $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
- $(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
- $(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2$
- (ax + b)(cx + d): multiply each term by each term (4 products).

**Formula:** `F-ALG-SQ` $(a\pm b)^2 = a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2$

### Worked example (Foundation Mastery) — MQ-G10C04-F-SQUARE_BIN-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C04-F-SQUARE_BIN-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C04", "area": "ALG", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Foundation Mastery", "difficulty": "easy", "formula": "F-ALG-SQ", "diagram": null, "kind": "algebra", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C04-F-SQUARE_BIN-01-S1", "MQ-G10C04-F-SQUARE_BIN-01-S2"]} -->
**Topic:** Binomial Expressions · **Pathway:** Foundation Mastery · **Difficulty:** easy · **Formula:** `F-ALG-SQ`

**Question:** Expand: $(x + 3)^2$

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Use (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b². <!-- MQ-G10C04-F-SQUARE_BIN-01-S1 -->

$$(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 2\times x\times 3 + 3^2$$

Step 2 — Simplify each term. <!-- MQ-G10C04-F-SQUARE_BIN-01-S2 -->

$$= x^{2} + 6x + 9$$

**Final answer:** $x^{2} + 6x + 9$

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Writing (x + a)² = x² + a² (missing the middle term).

## G10C05 · Grade 10 · Chapter 5 — Congruence of Triangles

<!-- meta {"id": "G10C05", "grade": 10, "chapter": 5, "area": "GEO", "sections": ["P1A", "P2B"], "medium": "en"} -->

**Exam sections:** Paper I — Part A, Paper II — Part B

Two triangles are congruent if they have exactly the same size and shape.

**Prerequisite:** Naming angles with three letters; properties of parallel lines.

### Key ideas

- Conditions: SAS, ASA, AAS, SSS, RHS.
- In SAS the angle must be BETWEEN the two sides.
- AAA and SSA do NOT prove congruence.
- After proving congruence, corresponding sides/angles are equal.

**Formula:** `T-GEO-CONG` $\text{SAS, ASA, AAS, SSS, RHS}$

**Diagram:** `GEO-ISOS-BISECT-01` `Shared_Assets/Geometry/GEO-ISOS-BISECT-01.svg`

### Worked example (Foundation Mastery) — MQ-G10C05-F-CONG_CONDI-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C05-F-CONG_CONDI-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C05", "area": "GEO", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Foundation Mastery", "difficulty": "easy", "formula": "T-GEO-CONG", "diagram": null, "kind": "concept", "verification": "logic-review", "steps": ["MQ-G10C05-F-CONG_CONDI-01-S1", "MQ-G10C05-F-CONG_CONDI-01-S2"]} -->
**Topic:** Congruence of Triangles · **Pathway:** Foundation Mastery · **Difficulty:** easy · **Formula:** `T-GEO-CONG`

**Question:** In triangles ABC and PQR: AB = PQ, ∠B = ∠Q, BC = QR. Which condition shows ΔABC ≡ ΔPQR?

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — List the equal parts in order around the triangle. <!-- MQ-G10C05-F-CONG_CONDI-01-S1 -->

$$\text{AB = PQ, ∠B = ∠Q, BC = QR}$$

Step 2 — Match the pattern to a condition. <!-- MQ-G10C05-F-CONG_CONDI-01-S2 -->

$$\Rightarrow \text{SAS}$$

**Final answer:** $SAS (ப.கோ.ப)$

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Using AAA — equal angles alone do not prove congruence.

The LCM of algebraic expressions is the simplest expression that each one divides into exactly.

**Prerequisite:** LCM of numbers; factorising (Chapter 7).

### Key ideas

- Factorise every expression completely.
