## P1B-FRAC — Fractions of a whole — multi-stage word problem

<!-- meta {"id": "P1B-FRAC", "section": "P1B", "chapters": ["G10C03"], "official_years": ["OL2018", "OL2019"], "moe_practice": ["MOE2024", "MOE2026"], "medium": "en"} -->
**Paper I — Part B** · Historical frequency: 2/2 official papers (OL2018, OL2019); MOE practice: MOE2024, MOE2026

- **How to recognise it:** A whole (money, land, production) is shared in stages; words like 'of the remainder', 'the rest'.
- **What the examiner asks:** Fractions of the whole, the remaining fraction, and finally the actual quantity.
- **Chapters / concepts:** `G10C03` Fractions
- **Formulas / theorems:** `F-NUM-FRAC`

### Standard solving technique

1. Write the first part as a fraction of the whole.
2. Find the remainder = 1 − that fraction.
3. Convert 'fraction of remainder' into a fraction of the whole (multiply).
4. Use the given amount to find the whole (divide by its fraction).

- **Common variations:** Sharing money among people; land for crops; production to markets; time spent on tasks.
- **Common mistakes:** Taking a fraction of the whole when the question says 'of the remainder'.
- **Examination working:** Show each fraction on its own line; marks are given for each stage.
- **Related past-paper IDs (File 06):** `PP-OL2019-PIB-Q01`, `PP-OL2018-PIB-Q01`, `PP-MOE2024-PIB-Q01`, `PP-MOE2026-PIB-Q01`

### Worked example — MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C03", "area": "NUM", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Grade Improvement", "difficulty": "medium", "formula": "F-NUM-FRAC", "diagram": null, "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S1", "MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S2", "MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S3", "MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S4"]} -->
**Topic:** Fractions · **Pathway:** Grade Improvement · **Difficulty:** medium · **Formula:** `F-NUM-FRAC`

**Question:** A man spent \frac{1}{3} of his salary of Rs. 120 000 on food and \frac{1}{5} of the REMAINDER on education. How much money is left?

- **Hint 1:** 'Of the remainder' means use the money left after food.
- **Hint 2:** Work in stages: food → remainder → education → left.

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Money for food. <!-- MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S1 -->

$$120\,000\times \frac{1}{3} = 40\,000$$

Step 2 — Remainder after food. <!-- MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S2 -->

$$120\,000 - 40\,000 = 80\,000$$

Step 3 — Education is a fraction of the remainder. <!-- MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S3 -->

$$80\,000\times \frac{1}{5} = 16\,000$$

Step 4 — Money left. <!-- MQ-G10C03-G-FRACTION_R-01-S4 -->

$$80\,000 - 16\,000 = 64\,000$$

**Final answer:** $\text{Rs. }64\,000$
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $\text{Rs. }64\,000$ · **Unit:** Rs.

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Taking the second fraction of the whole salary instead of the remainder.

## P1B-MENS — Composite plane figure — perimeter and area

<!-- meta {"id": "P1B-MENS", "section": "P1B", "chapters": ["G10C01", "G10C06"], "official_years": ["OL2019"], "moe_practice": ["MOE2024", "MOE2026"], "medium": "en"} -->
**Paper I — Part B** · Historical frequency: 1/2 official papers (OL2019); MOE practice: MOE2024, MOE2026

- **How to recognise it:** A diagram combining rectangles, trapeziums, semicircles or sectors; π = 22/7.
- **What the examiner asks:** Arc length, perimeter of the outline, area of parts and of the whole.
- **Chapters / concepts:** `G10C01` Perimeter, `G10C06` Area
- **Formulas / theorems:** `F-MEN-ARC`, `F-MEN-CIRC`, `F-MEN-SECTA`, `F-MEN-TRAP`

### Standard solving technique

1. Split the figure into known shapes.
2. Find missing lengths (radius = half the side, etc.).
3. Perimeter: only outside edges.
4. Area: add/subtract parts.

- **Common variations:** Garden plans, laminas, windows; sometimes a follow-up cost or number-of-items question.
- **Common mistakes:** Counting inside edges in the perimeter; using 2πr for area.
- **Examination working:** Write the formula before substituting; give units (cm, cm²).
- **Related past-paper IDs (File 06):** `PP-OL2019-PIB-Q02`, `PP-MOE2024-PIB-Q02`, `PP-MOE2026-PIB-Q02`

### Worked example — MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C01", "area": "MEN", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Grade Improvement", "difficulty": "medium", "formula": "F-MEN-CIRC", "diagram": "GEO-COMPOSITE-01", "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S1", "MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S2", "MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S3", "MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S4"]} -->
**Topic:** Perimeter · **Pathway:** Grade Improvement · **Difficulty:** medium · **Formula:** `F-MEN-CIRC`

**Question:** A shape is a rectangle 20 cm long and 14 cm wide, with a semicircle drawn outward on one 14 cm side (that side is removed). Find the perimeter of the shape. (π = 22/7)

**Diagram:** `GEO-COMPOSITE-01` → `Shared_Assets/Geometry/GEO-COMPOSITE-01.svg` (generic diagram — not drawn to scale; use the values in the question)

- **Hint 1:** Trace the outline: which edges are on the outside?
- **Hint 2:** The side under the semicircle is NOT part of the perimeter.

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Radius of the semicircle = half of the width. <!-- MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S1 -->

$$r = 14\div 2 = 7\ \text{cm}$$

Step 2 — Curved edge = half the circumference. <!-- MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S2 -->

$$\pi r = \frac{22}{7}\times 7 = 22\ \text{cm}$$

Step 3 — Add the straight edges: two lengths and one width. <!-- MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S3 -->

$$20+20+14 = 54\ \text{cm}$$

Step 4 — Total perimeter. <!-- MQ-G10C01-G-RECT_SEMIC-01-S4 -->

$$P = 54 + 22 = 76\ \text{cm}$$

**Final answer:** $76$ cm
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $76$ · **Unit:** cm

> ⚠️ **Common mistake:** Including the hidden side under the semicircle.

## P1B-FIN — Everyday financial calculations

<!-- meta {"id": "P1B-FIN", "section": "P1B", "chapters": ["G10C14", "G11C09", "G11C10"], "official_years": ["OL2018", "OL2019"], "moe_practice": ["MOE2024", "MOE2026"], "medium": "en"} -->
**Paper I — Part B** · Historical frequency: 2/2 official papers (OL2018, OL2019); MOE practice: MOE2024, MOE2026

- **How to recognise it:** Rates, income tax, customs duty, VAT, shares/dividends or simple interest in a short story.
- **What the examiner asks:** A percentage of the correct base, then totals or comparisons.
- **Chapters / concepts:** `G10C14` Percentages, `G11C09` Percentage, `G11C10` Share Market
- **Formulas / theorems:** `F-FIN-PROFIT`, `F-FIN-DISC`, `F-FIN-SI`, `F-FIN-RATES`, `F-FIN-CI`, `F-FIN-TAX`

### Standard solving technique

1. Identify the base (annual value, taxable income, CIF value…).
2. Apply the percentage.
3. Convert periods (annual → quarterly).
4. Answer exactly what is asked (amount, total, difference).

- **Common variations:** Rates + tax combined; shares bought with money left after duty.
- **Common mistakes:** Using the wrong base; forgetting quarterly payments.
- **Examination working:** Write 'Rs.' with every money answer.
- **Related past-paper IDs (File 06):** `PP-OL2019-PIB-Q03`, `PP-OL2018-PIB-Q03`, `PP-MOE2024-PIB-Q03`, `PP-MOE2026-PIB-Q03`

### Worked example — MQ-G10C14-G-DISCOUNT-01

**Topic:** Percentages · **Pathway:** Grade Improvement · **Difficulty:** medium · **Formula:** `F-FIN-DISC`

**Question:** The marked price of a bag is Rs. 7500. A discount of 15% is given. Find the selling price.

- **Hint 1:** Or: selling price = 85% of the marked price.

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Discount amount. <!-- MQ-G10C14-G-DISCOUNT-01-S1 -->

$$7500 - 1125 = 6375$$

**Final answer:** $\text{Rs. }6375$

## P1B-STAT — Data representation from a table

<!-- meta {"id": "P1B-STAT", "section": "P1B", "chapters": ["G10C11", "G10C26", "G11C15"], "official_years": ["OL2018", "OL2019"], "moe_practice": ["MOE2024", "MOE2026"], "medium": "en"} -->
**Paper I — Part B** · Historical frequency: 2/2 official papers (OL2018, OL2019); MOE practice: MOE2024, MOE2026

- **How to recognise it:** A frequency table (grouped or ungrouped) with tasks to complete it and draw a chart.
- **What the examiner asks:** Pie-chart angles, histogram and frequency polygon, cumulative frequency, median.
- **Chapters / concepts:** `G10C11` Data Representation, `G10C26` Frequency Distribution, `G11C15` Data Representation and Prediction
- **Formulas / theorems:** `F-STA-PIE`, `F-STA-MEAN`, `F-STA-AMEAN`, `F-STA-MEDIAN`, `F-STA-QUART`

### Standard solving technique

1. Complete the table first.
2. Choose scales that use the grid.
3. Plot accurately (polygon at mid-points; ogive at upper boundaries).
4. Read values from your graph.

- **Common variations:** Pie chart; histogram + polygon; cumulative frequency + median/quartiles.
- **Common mistakes:** Plotting the polygon at class boundaries; ogive at mid-values.
- **Examination working:** Label axes and show construction lines for readings.
- **Related past-paper IDs (File 06):** `PP-OL2019-PIB-Q04`, `PP-OL2018-PIB-Q05`, `PP-MOE2024-PIB-Q04`, `PP-MOE2026-PIB-Q04`

### Worked example — MQ-G10C11-G-PIE_VALUE-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C11-G-PIE_VALUE-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C11", "area": "STA", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Grade Improvement", "difficulty": "medium", "formula": "F-STA-PIE", "diagram": null, "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C11-G-PIE_VALUE-01-S1", "MQ-G10C11-G-PIE_VALUE-01-S2"]} -->
**Topic:** Data Representation · **Pathway:** Grade Improvement · **Difficulty:** medium · **Formula:** `F-STA-PIE`

**Question:** A pie chart shows how 120 families travel to work. The bus sector has an angle of 90°. How many families travel by bus?

- **Hint 1:** Each degree represents total ÷ 360 families.

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — Number = (angle ÷ 360) × total. <!-- MQ-G10C11-G-PIE_VALUE-01-S1 -->

$$\frac{90}{360}\times 120$$

Step 2 — Calculate. <!-- MQ-G10C11-G-PIE_VALUE-01-S2 -->

$$= 30$$

**Final answer:** $30$
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $30$

## P1B-PROB — Two-stage probability: grid and tree

<!-- meta {"id": "P1B-PROB", "section": "P1B", "chapters": ["G10C30", "G11C25"], "official_years": ["OL2018", "OL2019"], "moe_practice": ["MOE2024", "MOE2026"], "medium": "en"} -->
**Paper I — Part B** · Historical frequency: 2/2 official papers (OL2018, OL2019); MOE practice: MOE2024, MOE2026

- **How to recognise it:** Coins, dice, cards or bags; 'mark the sample space on the grid', 'complete the tree diagram'.
- **What the examiner asks:** Listing outcomes, probabilities of single and combined events.
- **Chapters / concepts:** `G10C30` Probability, `G11C25` Probability
- **Formulas / theorems:** `F-PRO-BASIC`, `F-PRO-COMP`, `F-PRO-INDEP`, `F-PRO-TREE`

### Standard solving technique

1. Mark the sample space (grid or list).
2. Circle favourable outcomes and count.
3. Tree: fractions on branches, each pair adds to 1.
4. Multiply along, add between.

- **Common variations:** With/without replacement; two different experiments.
- **Common mistakes:** Adding along branches; probabilities greater than 1.
- **Examination working:** Leave answers as fractions in simplest form.
- **Related past-paper IDs (File 06):** `PP-OL2019-PIB-Q05`, `PP-OL2018-PIB-Q04`, `PP-MOE2024-PIB-Q05`, `PP-MOE2026-PIB-Q05`

### Worked example — MQ-G10C30-G-PROB_COMPL-01
<!-- meta {"id": "MQ-G10C30-G-PROB_COMPL-01", "grade": 10, "chapter": "G10C30", "area": "PRO", "medium": "en", "pathway": "Grade Improvement", "difficulty": "medium", "formula": "F-PRO-COMP", "diagram": null, "kind": "numeric", "verification": "machine-checked", "steps": ["MQ-G10C30-G-PROB_COMPL-01-S1"]} -->
**Topic:** Probability · **Pathway:** Grade Improvement · **Difficulty:** medium · **Formula:** `F-PRO-COMP`

**Question:** The probability that it rains tomorrow is \frac{3}{8}. Find the probability that it does NOT rain.

- **Hint 1:** An event and its complement add to 1.

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 1 — P(not A) = 1 − P(A). <!-- MQ-G10C30-G-PROB_COMPL-01-S1 -->

$$1 - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$$

**Final answer:** $\frac{5}{8}$
<br>**Accepted answer formats:** $\frac{5}{8}$

**Step-by-step solution**

Step 2 — Read the logs from the tables. <!-- MQ-G10C20-G-LOG_PRODUC-01-S2 -->

$$P = \text{antilog}\ 1.6512 \approx 44.8$$
