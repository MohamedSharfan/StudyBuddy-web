Practical, valid techniques and section-by-section strategy for the GCE O/L Mathematics examination. No grade is guaranteed.

## Examination structure (verified)

| Section | Time | Questions | Choice | Marks | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper I — Part A | Paper I: 2 hours (Parts A and B together) | 25 short questions | All compulsory, answered on the paper | 2 marks each = 50 | 2018, 2019 official; MOE 2023(2024), 2025(2026) |
| Paper I — Part B | within the 2 hours of Paper I | 5 structured questions | All compulsory, answered on the paper | 10 marks each = 50 | 2018, 2019 official; MOE practice |
| Paper II — Part A | Paper II: 3 hours + 10 minutes reading (Parts A and B together) | 6 questions (Q1–6) | Answer any 5 | 10 marks each = 50 | 2018 (reading time), 2019 (3 h + 10 min); MOE practice |
| Paper II — Part B | within Paper II | 6 questions (Q7–12) | Answer any 5 | 10 marks each = 50 | 2018, 2019 official; MOE practice |

Calculators are not allowed (secondary source; confirm with the current DoE instructions). Mathematical tables are provided.


## Paper I — Part A

**Time management:** About 1 hour for 25 questions — roughly 2 minutes each.

- Answer on the paper; write one or two lines of working — 1 of the 2 marks is often for the method.
- Do the quick ones first (logs, LCM, factorising, probability), mark hard ones and return.
- Circle-angle questions: write the theorem name next to each angle.
- Last question has been a locus/construction sketch — draw neat construction lines.

Recurring question types (File 07): `P1A-PCT`, `P1A-FACT`, `P1A-LOG`, `P1A-RATE`, `P1A-CIRC`, `P1A-MENS`, `P1A-LINE`, `P1A-AFRAC`

## Paper I — Part B

**Time management:** About 1 hour for 5 questions — 10–12 minutes each.

- All five are compulsory and follow a stable order in verified papers: fractions → mensuration → finance → statistics → probability (2018 swapped the last two and used a distance–time graph for Q2).
- Each part (i), (ii), (iii) often uses the previous answer — if one part is wrong, still continue with your value.
- Graphs and charts: use a sharp pencil and the given scale.

Recurring question types (File 07): `P1B-FRAC`, `P1B-MENS`, `P1B-FIN`, `P1B-STAT`, `P1B-PROB`, `P1B-GRAPH`

## Paper II — Part A

**Time management:** Paper II: 3 hours for 10 questions → about 17 minutes each; use the 10 minutes reading time to choose.

- Choose 5 of 6. Verified papers always had: a finance question, a quadratic graph, algebra (simultaneous equations), a quadratic word problem or mensuration, trigonometry/bearings, and a grouped-mean question.
- The grouped-mean and graph questions are method-heavy and reliable for Foundation and Grade Improvement students.
- Start each question on a new page; number parts clearly.

Recurring question types (File 07): `P2A-FIN`, `P2A-QGRAPH`, `P2A-ALG`, `P2A-QEQ`, `P2A-TRIG`, `P2A-MENS`, `P2A-MEAN`

## Paper II — Part B

**Time management:** About 17 minutes per question; proofs can take longer — keep 15 minutes at the end to check.

- Choose 5 of 6. Verified papers had: progressions (Q7), constructions (Q8), two proof questions, a Venn diagram (Q11), and volume/trigonometry/tangent questions.
- Progressions, constructions and Venn diagrams are method-based — practise them until automatic.
- In proofs, every correct statement with a reason can earn marks even if you cannot finish.

Recurring question types (File 07): `P2B-PROG`, `P2B-CONS`, `P2B-PGEO`, `P2B-PCIR`, `P2B-TRIG`, `P2B-VENN`, `P2B-VOL`

## Valid shortcuts with worked examples

### TR-01 — Squares near a round number
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-01", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** Write the number as (round ± small) and use (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b².
- **Why it works:** It is just the binomial identity.
- **When to use:** Numbers close to 50, 100, 200…
- **When not to use:** Numbers far from a round number — normal multiplication is faster.
- **In the exam:** Valid; write the expansion line as working.

**Question:** Find 98².

Step 1 — Write 98 = 100 − 2.

$$98^2 = (100-2)^2$$

Step 2 — Expand.

$$= 10000 - 400 + 4$$

Step 3 — Calculate.

$$= 9604$$

**Final answer:** $9604$

### TR-02 — Difference of squares for products
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-02", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** If two numbers are equally spaced around a middle number m: (m + k)(m − k) = m² − k².
- **Why it works:** Expanding (m + k)(m − k) cancels the middle terms.
- **When to use:** 53 × 47, 102 × 98 …
- **When not to use:** When the middle number is awkward.
- **In the exam:** Valid; show m² − k².

**Question:** Find 53 × 47.

Step 1 — Middle number 50, distance 3.

$$53\times47 = (50+3)(50-3)$$

Step 2 — Use a² − b².

$$= 2500 - 9$$

Step 3 — Calculate.

$$= 2491$$

**Final answer:** $2491$

### TR-03 — Cancel 7 before multiplying by 22/7
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-03", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** When r (or r², rh) contains 7, cancel the 7 first.
- **Why it works:** Multiplication order does not matter.
- **When to use:** Circle, cylinder, cone calculations with π = 22/7.
- **When not to use:** When the question says π = 3.14.
- **In the exam:** Valid.

**Question:** Area of a circle of radius 14 cm (π = 22/7).

Step 1 — Write the formula.

$$\frac{22}{7}\times14\times14$$

Step 2 — Cancel 7 into 14.

$$= 22\times2\times14$$

Step 3 — Multiply.

$$= 616\ \text{cm}^2$$

**Final answer:** $616\ \text{cm}^2$

### TR-04 — Percentage multipliers
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-04", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** Increase by p% → multiply by (100 + p)/100; decrease → (100 − p)/100.
- **Why it works:** Original + p% of original = original × (1 + p/100).
- **When to use:** VAT, discount, profit, compound interest.
- **When not to use:** When the question asks for the increase itself (not the new total).
- **In the exam:** Valid; write the multiplier step.

**Question:** A price of Rs. 2400 increases by 15%. Find the new price.

Step 1 — Multiplier.

$$\frac{115}{100}$$

Step 2 — Multiply.

$$2400\times\frac{115}{100} = 24\times115$$

Step 3 — Calculate.

$$= 2760$$

**Final answer:** $\text{Rs. }2760$

### TR-05 — Compound interest by repeated multiplier
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-05", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** Multiply by (1 + R/100) once for each year.
- **Why it works:** Each year's amount becomes the next year's principal.
- **When to use:** 2–3 years at a simple rate like 10%.
- **When not to use:** If the rate changes each year, use a table.
- **In the exam:** Valid; examiners also accept year-by-year tables.

**Question:** Rs. 50 000 at 10% compound interest for 2 years: total amount?

Step 1 — Year 1.

$$50000\times1.1 = 55000$$

Step 2 — Year 2.

$$55000\times1.1 = 60500$$

**Final answer:** $\text{Rs. }60\,500$

### TR-06 — Check factorisation by substituting x = 1
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-06", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** Put x = 1 (or 2) into the original and your factors — the values must match.
- **Why it works:** Equal expressions give equal values for every x.
- **When to use:** After any factorising or expanding.
- **When not to use:** A match for one x does not prove it — try two values if unsure.
- **In the exam:** A private check only — do not write it as the answer.

**Question:** Check x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).

Step 1 — Original at x = 1.

$$1+5+6 = 12$$

Step 2 — Factors at x = 1.

$$3\times4 = 12\ \checkmark$$

**Final answer:** $\text{consistent}$

### TR-07 — Sum of an AP with n/2 (first + last)
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-07", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** If you know the last term, Sₙ = n/2 (a + l).
- **Why it works:** Pairing first and last terms gives equal pair sums.
- **When to use:** Last term is given or easy.
- **When not to use:** When only a, d, n are known — use the other formula.
- **In the exam:** Valid formula.

**Question:** Find 1 + 2 + 3 + … + 100.

Step 1 — a = 1, l = 100, n = 100.

$$S = \frac{100}{2}(1+100)$$

Step 2 — Calculate.

$$= 50\times101 = 5050$$

**Final answer:** $5050$

### TR-08 — 'At least one' using the complement
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-08", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** P(at least one) = 1 − P(none).
- **Why it works:** 'At least one' and 'none' are complements.
- **When to use:** Many outcomes are favourable.
- **When not to use:** When 'exactly one' is asked.
- **In the exam:** Valid; write 1 − P(none).

**Question:** Two fair dice are rolled. P(at least one six)?

Step 1 — P(no six on one die).

$$\frac56$$

Step 2 — P(no six on both).

$$\frac56\times\frac56 = \frac{25}{36}$$

Step 3 — Complement.

$$1 - \frac{25}{36} = \frac{11}{36}$$

**Final answer:** $\frac{11}{36}$

### TR-09 — Spot Pythagorean triples
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-09", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25 and their multiples.
- **Why it works:** Multiplying all sides by k keeps a² + b² = c².
- **When to use:** Right triangles with whole-number sides.
- **When not to use:** Non-right triangles; non-matching ratios.
- **In the exam:** Still show c² = a² + b² as working in exams.

**Question:** Legs 15 cm and 20 cm. Hypotenuse?

Step 1 — 15 = 5 × 3, 20 = 5 × 4 → 3-4-5 × 5.

$$c = 5\times5$$

Step 2 — Working line.

$$c^2 = 225 + 400 = 625 \Rightarrow c = 25$$

**Final answer:** $25\ \text{cm}$

### TR-10 — Estimate before using log tables
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-10", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** Round each number to 1 significant figure and calculate roughly.
- **Why it works:** Catches a wrong characteristic (decimal point error).
- **When to use:** Every log-table calculation.
- **When not to use:** Never replace the log working with the estimate.
- **In the exam:** A check only.

**Question:** Is 40.5 a sensible value for 3.24 × 12.5?

Step 1 — Round.

$$3\times 10 = 30\ \text{to}\ 3\times13 = 39$$

Step 2 — Compare.

$$40.5 \approx 40\ \checkmark$$

**Final answer:** $\text{sensible}$

### TR-11 — Turning point from symmetric table values
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-11", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** Find two x-values with the same y; the turning point x is halfway.
- **Why it works:** A parabola is symmetric about its axis.
- **When to use:** Quadratic graph questions in Paper II.
- **When not to use:** Not for straight lines.
- **In the exam:** Valid; also checks your table.

**Question:** For y = x² − 2x − 3, y = 5 at x = −2 and x = 4. Turning point x?

Step 1 — Halfway.

$$x = \frac{-2+4}{2} = 1$$

Step 2 — Substitute.

$$y = 1 - 2 - 3 = -4$$

**Final answer:** $(1,\,-4)$

### TR-12 — Back-substitute to verify solutions
<!-- meta {"id": "TR-12", "medium": "en", "verified": true} -->
- **How it works:** Put your answers back into the ORIGINAL equation(s).
- **Why it works:** A true solution makes both sides equal.
- **When to use:** Linear, simultaneous, quadratic equations.
- **When not to use:** —
- **In the exam:** Recommended; costs 30 seconds.

**Question:** Check x = 2, y = 3 for 2x + 3y = 13 and 3x − 2y = 0.

Step 1 — First equation.

$$2(2)+3(3) = 13\ \checkmark$$

Step 2 — Second equation.

$$3(2)-2(3) = 0\ \checkmark$$

**Final answer:** $\text{correct}$


## Justified partial-mark opportunities

- Write the formula first, then substitute — method marks are given even if arithmetic slips.
- Never leave a question blank: a correct first step (table, diagram, equation) is worth trying.
- Carry your own answer forward to later parts.
- Exact mark allocations inside a question come from the official marking scheme; StudyBuddy does not invent them.

## Answer verification

- Substitute answers back into equations.
- Estimate before log-table work.
- Check that probabilities are between 0 and 1, angles in a triangle total 180°, pie angles total 360°.
- Check lengths are positive and sensible.

## Rounding

- Round only at the end; keep 4-figure table values in working.
- Follow the instruction: 'to the nearest whole number', '1 d.p.', '3 s.f.'.
- Money: two decimal places or whole rupees as the question uses.

## Units

- Area cm², volume cm³, 1 L = 1000 cm³, 1 m = 100 cm.
- Convert to the same unit before calculating.
- Write units in the final answer.

## Graph drawing guidance

- Use the scale given; label axes.
- Plot with small crosses or dots; join quadratics with a smooth freehand curve.
- Show dashed reading lines for every value you read.
- Read to the accuracy of the grid (usually 0.1 or 0.2).

## Geometrical construction guidance

- Sharp pencil, firm compass, clean ruler.
- Never erase construction arcs.
- Draw a labelled rough sketch first.
- Measure answers to the nearest mm/degree.

## Mathematical proof guidance

- Copy the figure and mark the given facts.
- One statement per line with its reason in brackets.
- Name theorems (e.g. 'angles in the same segment').
- End with the exact statement asked for.

## Common examination mistakes (from all chapters)

- **Perimeter:** Leaving out the two radii of a sector.
- **Square Root:** Choosing the decimal just below without checking which is closer.
- **Fractions:** Adding denominators.
- **Binomial Expressions:** (x + 3)² = x² + 9 (middle term missing).
- **Congruence of Triangles:** Statements without reasons.
- **Area:** Using 2πr instead of πr².
- **Factors of Quadratic Expressions:** Right product, wrong signs.
- **Triangles I:** Using 360° for a triangle.
- **Triangles II:** Assuming a triangle is isosceles without evidence.
- **Inverse Proportions:** Using direct proportion by mistake.
- **Data Representation:** Calculating a percentage instead of an angle.
- **Least Common Multiple of Algebraic Expressions:** Taking the lowest power (that is the HCF).
- **Algebraic Fractions:** Multiplying only part of a numerator.
- **Percentages:** Dividing profit by the selling price.
- **Equations:** Multiplying only one side.
- **Parallelograms I:** Thinking diagonals are equal (true only for rectangles).
- **Parallelograms II:** Using only 'one pair parallel' (that is a trapezium).
- **Sets:** Not subtracting the intersection.
- **Logarithms I:** log(a + b) = log a + log b (false).
- **Logarithms II:** Treating $\bar{2}.3010$ as −2.3010.
- **Graphs:** (−3)² written as −9.
- **Rate:** Mixing minutes and hours.
- **Formulae:** Forgetting the square root step.
- **Arithmetic Progressions:** Using n instead of (n − 1).
- **Algebraic Inequalities:** Not reversing the sign.
- **Frequency Distribution:** Dividing by the number of classes.
- **Chords of a Circle:** Using the whole chord in Pythagoras.
- **Construction:** Changing compass width between arcs.
- **Surface Area and Volume:** Mixing area and volume units.
- **Probability:** Probability greater than 1.
- **Angles in a Circle:** Using the wrong arc.
- **Scale Diagrams:** Measuring bearings anticlockwise.
- **Real Numbers:** $\sqrt{a+b} = \sqrt a + \sqrt b$ (false).
- **Indices and Logarithms I:** Multiplying indices when multiplying powers.
- **Indices and Logarithms II:** Dividing a bar number directly.
- **Surface Area of Solids:** Using h instead of l.
- **Volume of Solids:** Forgetting ⅓.
- **Binomial Expressions:** (2x)³ = 2x³ (false; it is 8x³).
- **Algebraic Fractions:** Cancelling terms, e.g. (x + 2)/2 = x.
- **Areas of Plane Figures between Parallel Lines:** Using the slanted side as height.
- **Percentage:** Using simple interest for compound.
- **Share Market:** Using the dividend as a percentage of the wrong value.
- **Mid-point Theorem:** Doubling instead of halving.
- **Graphs:** Joining points with straight lines (draw a smooth curve).
- **Equations:** −b wrong when b is negative.
- **Equiangular Triangles:** Pairing non-corresponding sides.
- **Data Representation and Prediction:** Plotting at mid-values instead of upper boundaries.
- **Geometric Progressions:** Adding r instead of multiplying.
- **Pythagoras' Theorem:** Adding when finding a shorter side.
- **Trigonometry:** Wrong ratio for the sides given.
- **Matrices:** Multiplying element by element.
- **Inequalities:** Shading the wrong side.
- **Cyclic Quadrilaterals:** Thinking opposite angles are equal.
- **Tangents:** Missing the 90° at the point of contact.
- **Constructions:** Confusing circumcentre and incentre.
- **Sets:** Not subtracting the centre from pair regions.
- **Probability:** Adding along a branch.

## Preparation by pathway

### Foundation Mastery

- Master the method-based questions first: Paper I Part B fractions, probability, statistics; Paper II grouped mean, quadratic graph, constructions, AP, Venn.
- Practise prerequisite skills daily: fractions, negative numbers, squaring, substitution.
- Always write the formula and a first step — this builds partial marks.
- Track confidence: aim for steady progress, not perfection. Studying selected topics does not guarantee a pass.

### Grade Improvement

- Aim for consistent accuracy: re-check every calculation line.
- Learn to recognise question patterns quickly (File 07).
- Time yourself: 2 min per Part A question, 17 min per Paper II question.
- Keep an error log and redo wrong questions after a week.

### A Grade Mastery

- Do full papers under timed conditions; target finishing with 15 minutes to check.
- Master proofs and combined-topic questions (logs + volume; tangents + similar triangles).
- Use efficient methods (multipliers, identities) but keep full working.
- Verify every answer quickly with estimation or substitution.
